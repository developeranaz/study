# 1. FERROUS METALS — Part 1

## 1.1 Introduction

- **Ferrous metals**: metals with iron as main constituent. (Latin: *ferrum* = iron)
- Important ferrous metals: **Cast iron, Wrought iron, Pig iron, Steel**
- **Non-ferrous metals**: do not contain iron as main constituent. Examples: Aluminium, Copper.
- **Ore**: a solid, naturally occurring mineral aggregate from which one or more valuable constituents can be recovered by treatment.

---

## 1.2 Iron Ore

- Iron ore contains **compounds of iron + impurities**.
- Impurities in ore = **Gangue**
- Common impurities: Carbon, Manganese, Phosphorus, Silicon, Sulphur

### Types of Iron Ore

| Ore | Chemical Formula | Iron % | Specific Gravity | Colour | Location in India |
|-----|-----------------|--------|-----------------|--------|-------------------|
| **Magnetite** | Fe₃O₄ | 70–73% (richest) | 4.9–5.2 | Black | Madras |
| **Haematite** | Fe₂O₃ | 65–70% | 4.5–5.3 | Black / Steel grey | Mysore, Madhya Pradesh |
| **Limonite** | 2Fe₂O₃·3H₂O | 60% | 3.6–4.0 | Brown / Yellowish brown | Jamshedpur |
| **Pyrite** | FeS₂ | 45–47% | 4.8–5.1 | Bronze yellow / Pale brass yellow | — |
| **Siderite** | FeCO₃ | 40% | — | Pale yellow / Brownish red | Raniganj, West Bengal |

> **Mnemonic (Iron % descending): M-H-L-P-S → "Men Hostel L P School"**
> Magnetite > Haematite > Limonite > Pyrite > Siderite

> **Note:** Pyrite is **not used** for manufacturing iron because the sulphur content makes it **brittle**.  
> Siderite is also known as **Spathic iron ore**.

---

## 1.3 Pig Iron

- **Definition**: The crude, impure iron extracted from iron ores is called Pig Iron.
- **Carbon content**: 5–6%
- Pig iron is the **basic raw material** for manufacturing Cast iron, Wrought iron, and Steel.

### Manufacturing Process of Pig Iron

Three main stages:

**1. Dressing**
- Iron ore from mines is crushed to pieces of ~25 mm diameter.
- Washed with water or cleaned using magnetic separators to remove impurities.

**2. Calcination & Roasting**

| Process | Definition |
|---------|-----------|
| **Calcination** | Heating ore below its melting point in absence/limited air; removes water and CO₂ |
| **Roasting** | Heating ore below its melting point with excess air; dissipates volatile parts (especially Sulphur) |

**3. Smelting**
- Extraction of metal from ore by heating and melting.
- Carried out in a **Blast Furnace**.
- Blast furnace dimensions: **30–40 mm thick walls, 6–8 m diameter, 30 m height**.

# 2. FERROUS METALS — Part 2

## 2.1 Parts of a Blast Furnace

[Diagram: Vertical cross-section of blast furnace showing throat (top) → stack → cylinder/barrel → bosh → hearth (bottom), with tuyeres at bosh level]

| Part | Location | Function |
|------|----------|----------|
| **Throat** | Top — feeding zone/mouth | Receives iron ore + flux (limestone) + fuel (coke); has bell & cone / double cup & cone arrangement |
| **Stack (Inwall)** | Below throat; height ~18 m | Inclined section connecting throat to barrel; angle 85°–87° |
| **Cylinder / Barrel** | Connects stack and bosh | Height-to-diameter ratio = 4.0 to 4.5; Total furnace height 30–36 m, diameter 6–8 m |
| **Bosh** | Between cylinder and hearth | **Hottest / burning zone**; inward slope 76°–80°; height = 15–20% of total furnace height |
| **Hearth / Crucible** | Lowest part | Collects iron and slag; height 2–3 m |
| **Tuyeres** | Located at bosh portion | Nozzle that blasts air into the furnace |

---

## 2.2 Properties of Pig Iron

- Does not rust
- Strong in **compression**, weak in **tension and shear**
- Hard and brittle
- Cannot be magnetised, welded, or riveted
- Fusible temperature: **1200°C**

### Varieties of Pig Iron

| Variety | Colour | Key Properties | Use |
|---------|--------|---------------|-----|
| **Bessemer pig iron** | — | From Haematite ore | Manufacture of steel |
| **Grey pig iron (Foundry pig iron)** | Grey | Soft; burnt at high temp | Cast-iron castings |
| **White pig iron (Forged pig iron)** | White | Hard and strong; burnt at low temp | Manufacture of wrought iron; unfit for superior casting |
| **Mottled pig iron** | Mottled | Intermediate between grey & white; large proportion of combined carbon | Heavy foundry castings; unfit for light/ornamental castings |

---

## 2.3 Cast Iron

- Made by re-melting pig iron with coke and limestone in a **Cupola furnace**.
- **Cupola furnace** is cylindrical: diameter ~1 m, height ~5 m.
- **Carbon content**: 2–4%
- Impurities: Manganese, Phosphorus, Silicon, Sulphur

### Properties of Cast Iron
- Hard and brittle
- Becomes soft when placed in salt water
- Does not rust
- Cannot be magnetised, welded, or riveted
- Melting temperature: **1250°C**
- Specific gravity: **7.5**

### Uses of Cast Iron
- Sewers, cisterns, water pipes, rail chairs

### Varieties of Cast Iron

| Variety | Description | Use |
|---------|------------|-----|
| **Grey cast iron** | Made from grey pig iron; soft, weak, grey colour | Extensively used in castings |
| **White cast iron** | Silvery white; hard | — |
| **Mottled cast iron** | Intermediate between grey and white | Small castings |
| **Chilled cast iron** | Hard outside, soft inside | — |
| **Malleable cast iron** | Carbon partially extracted; becomes malleable | Railway equipment, automobiles, pipe fittings, agricultural implements, door fastenings, hinges |
| **Ductile cast iron** | Manganese treatment given; spheroidal graphite iron | — |
| **Toughened cast iron** | Obtained by melting cast iron with wrought iron scrap (1/4 to 1/7 of cast iron weight) | — |

# 3. GLASS

## 3.1 Introduction

- Glass is a **solidified supercooled solution** of a number of metallic silicates.
- **Amorphous** (no crystalline structure), **transparent** and **translucent**
- Transparent and **insulating** due to **absence of free electrons**
- Brittle material
- **Melting point**: 1400–1500°C
- **Density**: 24.3 kN/m³
- **Compressive strength**: 700–1000 N/mm²
- **Tensile strength**: 30–60 N/mm²

### General Composition Formula
> **aX₂O · bYO · cSiO₂**
- X = atom of alkali metal (Na or K)
- Y = atom of bivalent metal (Calcium, Lead)

---

## 3.2 Constituents of Glass

| Constituent | Form Used | Function |
|-------------|----------|----------|
| **Silica** (major constituent) | Quartz / crushed sandstone | Main glass-forming material; carbonates of K and Na added to lower fusing temp |
| **Lime** | Limestone / chalk | Provides durability and toughness |
| **Soda** | — | Acts as accelerator for fusion; excess soda is harmful |
| **Potash** | — | Makes glass fire resistant and infusable |
| **Lead Oxide** | 15–30% replaces lime | Imparts colour, brightness, shine; lowers melting point; increases workability; but loses transparency and makes glass brittle |
| **Cullets** | Broken glass pieces | Acts as flux; helps fuse easily; reduces cost |
| **Iron** | Undesirable impurity | Gives bottle-green colour; Manganese dioxide added to remove this colour |

---

## 3.3 Types of Glass

| Type | Chemical Formula | Key Properties | Uses |
|------|-----------------|---------------|------|
| **Soda lime glass** (Soft/Commercial glass) | Na₂O·CaO·6SiO₂ | Mixture of sodium & calcium silicates; fuses at low temperature; melts easily; clear state | Window glass, plate glass, lab apparatus |
| **Potash lime glass** (Bohemian / Hard glass) | K₂O·CaO·6SiO₂ | Contains potassium & calcium silicates; fuses at high temperature; not easily attacked by water/solvents | Articles requiring high temperatures; combustion tubes |
| **Potash lead glass** (Flint glass) | — | Mixture of potassium silicate & lead silicate; fuses very easily; bright lustre; turns black/opaque near furnace gas; affected by aqueous solutions | Artificial gems, electric bulbs, lenses, prisms |
| **Common glass / Bottle glass** | — | Mixture of sodium, calcium & iron silicates; fuses with difficulty; brown/yellow colour | Medicine bottles |
| **Borosilicate glass (Pyrex)** | 70–80% silica + 7–13% boric acid + small amounts of alkali and aluminium oxide | Good chemical durability; excellent thermal shock resistance | Chemical industry, textile industry, lab apparatus |

---

## 3.4 Manufacturing of Glass

Five steps:

**1. Collection of Raw Materials**
- Type collected depends on type of glass required.
- Materials include: raw materials + cullets + decolourizers
- Ferrous oxide → green colour; Ferric oxide → yellowish colour
- Common decolourizers: Antimony oxide, Arsenic oxide, Cobalt oxide, Manganese oxide, Nickel oxide

**2. Batch Preparation**
- Raw materials, cullets, and decolourizers are finely powdered and accurately weighed in correct proportions.
- Mixed uniformly → product called **batch** or **frit**.

**3. Melting**
| Furnace Type | Construction | Scale |
|-------------|-------------|-------|
| **Pot furnace** | Vessel of fire clay / refractory clay | Small-scale production |
| **Tank furnace** | Constructed with reinforced masonry | Large-scale production |

**4. Fabrication**
- Molten glass given suitable shape by hand or machine.
- Methods: Blowing, Casting, Drawing, Pressing, Rolling, Spinning

**5. Annealing**
- Process of **slow and homogeneous cooling** of glass articles (prevents internal stresses).

| Method | Scale |
|--------|-------|
| Flue treatment | Large-scale production |
| Oven treatment | Small-scale production |

---

## 3.5 Special Varieties of Glass

| Variety | Description | Use |
|---------|------------|-----|
| **Bullet proof glass** | Several layers of plate glass with alternate layers of vinyl resin plastic; thickness 15–75 mm | Bullet-resistant applications |
| **Fibre glass** | Soft, flexible, high strength-to-weight ratio, does not absorb water | Communication technology, endoscopy, optical fibres, FRP |
| **Float glass / Plate glass** | Molten glass floated on molten tin; energy-efficient glass | Residential buildings, commercial complexes, furniture |
| **Foam glass** | Finely ground glass + carbon melted together; expands to black foam; fire proof, rigid | Air conditioning, refrigeration (substitute for cork); floats in water (low density) |
| **Heat excluding glass (Anti-actinic glass)** | Allows light, eliminates heat | Railway coach windows, important building window panels |
| **Obscured glass** | Transmits light but obscures vision | Public toilets, office doors, partitions |
| **Perforated glass** | Perforations made by rollers | Ventilator panels |
| **Safety glass (Shatter-proof / Toughened glass)** | Celluloid sheet placed between plate glass with glue | Automobiles |
| **Shield glass** | Contains heavy elements like lead oxide | Windows where high radiation is observed |
| **Soluble glass** | Soluble in water under normal conditions | Acid-resistant cement |
| **Structural glass (Glass bricks)** | — | Pavement lights, partitions, lantern lights |
| **Ultra-violet ray glass** | Transmits UV rays even when not directed at sun | Windows of schools, hospitals |
| **Wired glass** | Steel wire mesh placed between plate glass during rolling | Roofs, fire-resisting doors and windows |
| **Alkali barium silicate glass** | Soft glass; excellent glass-to-metal sealing | TV sets (filters X-rays) |
| **Alumino silicate glass** | Resists high temp up to 750°C | Halogen lamps, compression tubes, steam boilers |

# 4. AGGREGATES

## 4.1 Introduction

- Aggregates derived from **igneous, sedimentary, and metamorphic rocks**, or manufactured from clays, slag, etc.
- Must be: **hard, strong, durable**, and free from clay, loam, vegetation and other foreign matter.
- Clay/dirt coating on aggregates **prevents cement adhesion** → retards setting/hardening → reduces strength, durability, and soundness of concrete.

---

## 4.2 Classification by Size

| Type | Definition | Notes |
|------|-----------|-------|
| **Fine Aggregate** | Most of material passes through **4.75 mm IS sieve** | Should not contain more than 1–8% fine particles; may be from sea, river, lake, or pit (free from impurities) |
| **Coarse Aggregate** | Particles **retained on 4.75 mm IS sieve** | Common materials: crushed hard stone, gravel; obtained by crushing granite, gneiss, crystalline limestone, sandstone |

### Maximum Size of Coarse Aggregate
- Mass concrete (dams, etc.): up to **63 mm** for plain concrete
- Reinforced cement concrete: generally **≤ 20 mm**; also must be **≥ 5 mm less** than clear spacing between reinforcement AND at least 5 mm less than clear cover.

---

## 4.3 Shape & Texture Effects on Mix

| Shape | Effect on Mix |
|-------|--------------|
| **Round** | Highly workable mix; minimum surface area to lubricate |
| **Angular** | High strength mix; stronger interparticle bond |
| **Flaky** | Poor mix — neither strong nor workable |

| Texture | Effect |
|---------|--------|
| **Smooth** | Higher workability |
| **Rough** | Higher strength |

---

## 4.4 Grading of Aggregates

**Purpose**: Proportion fine and coarse aggregates so as to get **strongest and densest mix with least cement**, minimum voids, and easy workability.

### Methods of Grading

| Method | Description |
|--------|------------|
| **Trial method** | Proportion that gives heaviest weight for same volume = densest concrete |
| **Fineness Modulus method (Sieve analysis)** | Sample passed through 9 standard sieves (150 micron to 80 mm); sum of cumulative % retained ÷ 100 = Fineness Modulus (FM) |
| **Minimum voids method** | Voids in fine and coarse aggregates found separately using graduated cylinder and water; X = (V₁–V₂)/V₂ × 100 |
| **Arbitrary standards** | Cement : Sand : Coarse aggregate = 1 : n : 2n; for small/moderate works only; not for large/important works |

> **FM Formula**: FM = (Sum of cumulative % retained on all standard sieves) / 100
>
> **Combining aggregates**: Proportion of fine aggregate (p) = (FM_coarse – FM_mix) / (FM_coarse – FM_fine)

### IS Grading Zones for Fine Aggregates (IS: 383)
- Four zones: **Zone I to Zone IV** — grading becomes progressively finer from Zone I → Zone IV.

---

## 4.5 Sand — Sources

| Source | Description | Suitability |
|--------|------------|-------------|
| **Pit sand** | From pits 1–2 m deep; sharp angular grains; free from salts | Good for mortar if free from organic matter and clay |
| **River sand** | From river beds; fine rounded grains; almost white; usually clean | Widely used for all purposes |
| **Sea sand** | From sea shores; rounded grains; light brown; contains salts → dampness, efflorescence, disintegration | **Not recommended** for engineering works; only after thorough washing |

### Characteristics of Good Sand
- Chemically inert
- Clean and coarse; free from organic matter
- Sharp, angular, and durable grains
- No salts
- Well graded (particles of various sizes in suitable proportions)

---

## 4.6 Sand Grading Classification

| Type | Screen Opening | Common Use |
|------|---------------|------------|
| **Fine sand** | 1.5875 mm | Masonry works |
| **Coarse sand** | 3.175 mm | Masonry work |
| **Gravelly sand** | 7.62 mm | Plastering |

---

## 4.7 Bulking of Sand

- Moisture in sand causes a **film of water** around particles → increases volume.
- For moisture content of **5–8%**, increase in volume = **5–8%** (varies with grading).
- **Finer the material → greater the bulking** for same moisture content.
- **Correction for bulking** must be done when volume batching is used.
- At 0% moisture (dry) and at high moisture (saturated), bulking is zero or negligible.

---

## 4.8 Testing of Aggregates

| Test | What it Measures |
|------|-----------------|
| **Aggregate Crushing Value** | Resistance to crushing under gradually applied load |
| **Aggregate Impact Value** | Resistance to sudden impact/shock |
| **Aggregate Abrasion Value** | Resistance to abrasion/wear |
| **Fineness Modulus (Sieve Analysis)** | Mean particle size; index of fineness/coarseness |
| **Flakiness Index** | Proportion of flaky particles |

# 5. ADMIXTURES

## 5.1 Definition

> **Admixture**: Anything added to concrete other than water, cement, sand, and coarse aggregates in order to **modify the properties** of concrete.

---

## 5.2 Types of Admixtures

### 1. Plasticizers
- Organic or organic+inorganic substances that either:
  - Reduce water needed for same workability → **high strength concrete**
  - Increase workability at same water-cement ratio → **high workability concrete**
- Are **deflocculating agents** — break up cement particle clusters that trap water.
- Normal dose: **0.1–0.4%** of cement weight
- Water reduction: up to **10–15%**
- Examples: Lignosulphate, Polyglycol esters, Hydroxylated carboxylic acid

### 2. Super Plasticizers
- Modified versions of plasticizers with greater effectiveness.
- Water reduction: up to **30%** (vs. 15% for plasticizers).
- Examples: Modified lignosulphate, Sulphonated melamine formaldehyde, Naphthalene formaldehyde

### 3. Retarders
- Slow down the **hydration** process → keep concrete plastic and workable longer.
- Useful in **hot weather concreting** (counteracts accelerating effect of heat).
- Normal dose: **0.05–0.1%**
- Examples: Calcium sulphate, Sugar, Starch, Cellulose

### 4. Accelerators
- Increase the **rate of gain of strength**.
- Used in: cold weather concreting, precast construction, emergency road work, fast form reuse.
- Examples:
  - **Calcium Chloride (CaCl₂)** — up to **2% by weight of cement** acts as accelerator; beyond 2% acts as **retarder**
  - Sodium chloride, Silicates, Fluoro silicates, Triethanolamine (up to 0.06%)

### 5. Air-Entraining Admixtures
- Entrap millions of tiny air bubbles in cement voids → bubbles act as **flexible ball bearings**.
- Improve: plasticity, workability, bleeding, segregation, permeability, frost resistance.
- Examples: Natural wood resins, plant and animal fats and oils, Hydrogen peroxide, Aluminium powder

---

## 5.3 Admixtures Comparison Table

| Type | Water Reduction | Dose | Key Examples |
|------|----------------|------|-------------|
| Plasticizer | Up to 15% | 0.1–0.4% | Lignosulphate, polyglycol esters |
| Super Plasticizer | Up to 30% | — | Sulphonated melamine, naphthalene formaldehyde |
| Retarder | — | 0.05–0.1% | Calcium sulphate, sugar, starch |
| Accelerator | — | CaCl₂ ≤ 2%; TEA ≤ 0.06% | CaCl₂, silicates, TEA |
| Air-entraining | — | — | Wood resins, aluminium powder |

# 6. CONCRETE — Part 1

## 6.1 Introduction

- **Concrete**: composite man-made material; most widely used building material in construction.
- Mixture of: **binding material** (lime or cement) + **coarse and fine aggregate** + **water** + sometimes **admixtures**.

### Functions of Each Component

| Component | Function |
|-----------|----------|
| **Cement** | Imparts adhesive and cohesive properties; binds all ingredients into a compact whole |
| **Aggregate** | Forms the body of concrete; occupies 70–80% of concrete volume |
| **Water** | Causes hydration of cement |

---

## 6.2 Classification of Concrete

### 1. Based on Cementing Material
- Lime concrete
- Gypsum concrete
- Cement concrete

### 2. Based on Specification Type

| Type | Description |
|------|------------|
| **Nominal Mix** | Specified by proportions by weight (e.g. 1:1.5:3 = cement:FA:CA); IS 456 restricts nominal mix to **M20 grade and below** |
| **Design Mix** | Concrete properties (strength, w/c ratio, workability etc.) are specified; designed to achieve required characteristic strength |

### 3. Based on Grade (IS 456)

| Strength Group | Range (N/mm²) | Examples |
|---------------|--------------|---------|
| **Low strength** | < 20 N/mm² | M10, M15 |
| **Medium strength** | 20–40 N/mm² | M20, M25, M30 |
| **High strength** | > 40 N/mm² | M45, M50, ... |

> Grade M20 = characteristic compressive strength of **20 MPa** (150 mm cube, 28 days)

### 4. Based on Place of Casting

| Type | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **In-situ concrete** | Concrete placed in position at site |
| **Precast concrete** | Concrete used to make prefabricated units in a factory |

---

## 6.3 Manufacturing of Concrete — 7 Stages

1. Batching
2. Mixing
3. Transporting
4. Placing
5. Compacting
6. Curing
7. Finishing

---

## 6.4 Stage 1: Batching

- **Definition**: Controlling the quantity of each material required for making a concrete mix in each batch.
- Accuracy: **±3%** for aggregates, cement, and water; **±5%** for admixtures.

### Methods

| Method | Description | When Used |
|--------|------------|----------|
| **Volume batching** | Each solid ingredient measured by loose volume using a **gauge box (farma)** | Small jobs only; correction for bulking of sand required |
| **Weight batching** | Materials weighed; water in kg or litres (1 litre = 1 kg) | **Recommended for important works**; bulking effect of sand not an issue; surface water of wet aggregates must be accounted for |

- **Cement always measured by weight** regardless of batching method.
- Volume of 1 bag of cement = **0.035 m³** (35 litres)

---

## 6.5 Stage 2: Mixing

- Objective: obtain **homogeneous, uniform colour**, consistent concrete of desired strength.
- IS 456 recommended mixing time: **~2 minutes**
- Approximately **20 revolutions** of mixer drum provides sufficient mixing.
- Too little mixing → poor quality; too much → uneconomical.

### Methods

| Method | When Used |
|--------|----------|
| **Hand mixing** | Small jobs with small quantity; mixing time ~2 min, max 3 min |
| **Machine mixing** | Large quantities; faster rate; better quality |

### Machines for Mixing

- **Batching plant** — has all arrangements for receiving raw materials from storage bins, weighing each ingredient, and mixing.
- Continuous mixers
- Tilting type mixer
- Non-tilting type mixer

---

# 7. CONCRETE — Part 2

## 7.1 Stage 3: Transporting

- **Time limit**: The entire process of mixing → transporting → placing → compacting must be completed within **initial setting time of cement = 30 minutes (OPC)**.

### Methods of Transporting Concrete
1. Pans
2. Wheel barrows / hand buggies
3. Power buggies
4. Tower bucket
5. Crane bucket
6. Dump truck
7. Chutes
8. Concrete pumps
9. Transit mixer
10. Agitating truck
11. Belt conveyor

---

## 7.2 Stage 4: Placing

- **Maximum permissible free fall of concrete** (as per IRC 456): **1.5 m**

---

## 7.3 Stage 5: Compaction

- **Definition**: Process of removal of entrapped air and uniform placement of concrete to form a **homogeneous dense mass**.
- Density, strength, and durability of concrete depend on quality of compaction.

### Effect of Voids on Strength

| Voids in Hardened Concrete | Decrease in Compressive Strength |
|---------------------------|--------------------------------|
| 5% | ~30% |
| 10% | ~60% |

---

## 7.4 Stage 6: Curing

- **IS 456 definition**: Curing is the process of **preventing loss of moisture** from concrete while maintaining a satisfactory temperature regime.
- Essential for **optimum strength and durability**.

### Strength Development During Curing

| Curing Condition | Strength Outcome |
|-----------------|-----------------|
| Moist cured for 7 days | ~50% stronger than concrete exposed to dry air for entire period |
| Cured for 1 month | ~double the strength of concrete exposed to dry air |
| Increase from 3 to 7 days | Very rapid |
| Beyond 3 weeks | Continues slowly for indefinite period |

- **C₃S** contributes to major part of initial strength; partially aided by **C₂S**; completed in ~3 weeks.

### Test Specimen Storage Conditions
- Free from vibration
- Moist air: **≥ 90% relative humidity**
- Temperature: **27 ± 2°C** for 24 hours

### Curing Duration (IS 456)

| Cement Type | Minimum Curing Period |
|------------|----------------------|
| OPC | 7 days at 90% humidity |
| Mineral admixtures / Blended cements | At least 10 days |

### Curing Temperature Range
- **5°C to 28°C**
- Lower temperature → reduces rate of setting
- Higher temperature → reduces ultimate strength

### Methods of Curing

| Method | Notes |
|--------|-------|
| Shading concrete work | Prevents direct sunlight evaporation |
| Wet hessian / gunny bags | Covers surface with wet material |
| Sprinkling water | Continuous water application on surface |
| **Ponding** | **Best method; generally used in practice** |
| Steam curing | **Recommended for precast concrete members** |
| Curing compounds | Applied as membrane on surface |

---

## 7.5 Maturity of Concrete

- Strength of concrete depends on both **age (period of curing)** and **temperature during curing**.
- **Maturity = Σ (time × temperature)**
- Maturity concept allows comparison of strength development under different temperature-time histories.

---

## 7.6 Stage 7: Finishing

- **Definition**: Process of levelling and smoothing the top surface of freshly placed concrete to achieve the desired appearance.

---

## 7.7 Strength of Concrete

### Grading / Characteristic Strength
- Grade denoted by letter **'M'** + number (e.g. **M20**)
- Number = characteristic compressive strength of **150 mm cube at 28 days** in MPa (N/mm²)
- **Characteristic strength**: strength below which **not more than 5%** of test results are expected to fail.

### Relative Strengths (approximate)

| Type | % of Compressive Strength |
|------|--------------------------|
| Tensile strength | ~10% |
| Bending strength | ~15% |
| Shear strength | ~20% |

> *No mathematical relation between compressive, tensile, bending, and shear strengths exists.*

---

## 7.8 Factors Affecting Strength

### 1. Water-Cement Ratio (Abram's Law)
- Strength of workable concrete depends **only on water-cement ratio**.
- In hardened state: **strength ∝ 1 / (w/c ratio)** — inversely proportional.

### 2. Size of Specimen
- 150 mm cube has **~25% more strength** than 150 mm dia × 300 mm height cylinder.
- Smaller cube size → higher compressive strength.

### 3. Air Voids
- Strength decreases with increase in percentage of air voids.
