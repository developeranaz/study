## 1. Introduction & Basic Facts

**Definition:** Cement is a material having adhesive and cohesive properties, capable of bonding material fragments into a compact mass.

**History:**
- Natural cement: made by crushing stone; brown in colour; also called Roman cement.
- Joseph Aspedin introduced **Portland Cement in 1824**.
- First cement factory in India: **Chennai, 1904** (South India Industries Ltd).
- First cement factory in Kerala: **Kottayam, 1956** (Travancore Cement Limited — white cement factory).

**Key Physical Data:**

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Specific gravity | 3.15 |
| Standard (bulk) density | 1440 kg/m³ |
| Weight of one bag | 50 kg |
| Volume of one bag | 0.035 m³ |
| Volume–weight relation | 1 ft³ = 42 kg cement |
| Bags per m³ | 30 bags |

## 2. Chemical Composition of Cement

**Raw Materials:**
- **Calcareous materials** (CaO): Limestone, chalk, marine shells, cement rock
- **Argillaceous materials** (Al₂O₃): Shale, clay (silica + alumina), marl, blast furnace slag, cement rock

**Composition Table:**

| Compound | Formula | Percentage | Typical |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lime | CaO | 62–65% | 63% |
| Silica | SiO₂ | 17–25% | 22% |
| Alumina | Al₂O₃ | 3–8% | 6% |
| Iron Oxide | Fe₂O₃ | 3–4% | — |
| Gypsum | CaSO₄ | 3–4% | — |
| Magnesia | MgO | 1–3% | — |
| Sulphur | S | 1–3% | — |
| Alkalies | — | 0.2–1% | — |

**Chemical Requirements (IS 269-1998):**

| Parameter | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Ratio of Alumina / Iron Oxide | > 0.66 |
| Lime Saturation Factor | 0.66 – 1.02 (not less than 0.66, not more than 1.02) |
| Total loss on ignition | ≤ 4% |
| Total sulphur content | ≤ 2.75% |
| Weight of insoluble residue | ≤ 1.50% |
| Weight of magnesia | ≤ 5% |

## 3. Functions of Each Compound

| Compound | Function | Effect of Excess | Effect of Deficiency |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lime (CaO)** | Binding, strength, soundness | Disintegration, unsoundness, expansion | Reduces strength, quick set, cracks |
| **Silica (SiO₂)** | Strength | Increases setting time (slow set) | Reduces strength |
| **Alumina (Al₂O₃)** | Initial setting; acts as flux (lowers burning temp) | Weakens cement | Sets slowly |
| **Gypsum (CaSO₄)** | Retarder — increases initial setting time | — | Flash set (uncontrolled rapid set) |
| **Iron Oxide (Fe₂O₃)** | Colour (reddish brown tint), strength, hardness | — | — |
| **Magnesia (MgO)** | Colour (yellowish tint), strength | Unsound cement | — |
| **Sulphur** | Soundness | — | — |
| **Alkalies** | (Accelerates setting) | Efflorescence, staining, white-grey spots on structure | — |

> **Note:** Soundness due to lime and magnesia can be measured directly by tests. However, **no test is available** to measure soundness due to sulphur directly.

## 4. Hydration of Cement

**Hydration** = chemical reaction between cement and water.

- It is an **exothermic reaction** (heat is released).
- Heat released is called **Heat of Hydration**.

**Water Requirements for Hydration:**

| Type | Purpose | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Bound water | Chemical reaction of cement compounds | 23% |
| Gel water | Fills pores initially | 15% |
| **Total** | Complete hydration | **38%** |

**Factors Affecting Hydration:**
1. **Fineness** — finer cement → more surface area → faster hydration
2. **Temperature** — higher temperature → faster hydration

**Heat of Hydration Limits (Normal OPC):**
- ≤ 65 cal/g at 7 days
- ≤ 75 cal/g at 28 days

## 5. Bogue's Compounds

These four compounds are formed during the burning of clinker in the rotary kiln. They are called **Bogue's compounds**.

| Compound | Chemical Name | Shorthand | Nickname | % Range | Key Role |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C₃S | Tricalcium Silicate | C₃S | **Alite** | 25–50% | Early strength (within 1 week) |
| C₂S | Dicalcium Silicate | C₂S | **Belite** | 25–40% | Ultimate/progressive strength (after 1 week) |
| C₃A | Tricalcium Aluminate | C₃A | **Celite** | 5–11% | Initial set (24 hrs); controlled by gypsum |
| C₄AF | Tetracalcium Aluminoferrite | C₄AF | **Felite** | 8–14% | Poor cementing value |

**Important Orders:**

| Category | Order |
|---|---|
| % Composition (high → low) | C₃S > C₂S > C₄AF > C₃A *(or C₃S > C₂S > C₃A > C₄AF)* |
| Order of Setting & Hydration (fastest first) | C₃A > C₃S > C₂S > C₄AF |
| Heat of Hydration (J/g) | C₃A (865) > C₃S (500) > C₄AF (420) > C₂S (260) |
| Rate of Hydration (k value, speed) | C₄AF > C₃A > C₃S > C₂S |

**Mnemonic — Nicknames (Alite, Belite, Celite, Felite):**
> **A**lite = C₃**S** (early **S**trength)  
> **B**elite = C₂**S** (ultimate **S**trength — **B**ig time later)  
> **C**elite = C₃**A** (controls with gypsum — **A**luminate)  
> **F**elite = C₄**AF** (poor value — last **F**inisher)

## 6. Manufacturing of Cement

**Basic formula:**  
Limestone + Fine Clay + Water → CEMENT (burned at ~1450°C)

OPC is manufactured by two processes:

### 6.1 Dry Process (New / Modern Method)
1. Raw materials (limestone + clay) are crushed and dried.
2. Mixed in correct proportions; stored in silos as raw mix.
3. Passed into rotary kiln for burning.

### 6.2 Wet Process (Old Method)
1. Raw materials are mixed with water to form slurry.
2. Slurry is fed into the rotary kiln.

### 6.3 Burning in Rotary Kiln

The rotary kiln is a **steel tube with refractory lining**. Three zones:

| Zone | What Happens |
|---|---|
| **Zone 1 — Dry Zone** | Water evaporates from the slurry |
| **Zone 2 — Pre-heating Zone** | CO₂ liberated by calcination; nodules are formed |
| **Zone 3 — Burning Zone** | Nodules → small, hard, dark greenish-blue balls called **clinkers**; Bogue's compounds form here |

- Burning temperature: **1400–1500°C**
- Hot clinkers (~1000°C) are cooled to **95°C** by a cooling mechanism.

### 6.4 Grinding
- Clinkers are finely ground in **ball mills and tube mills**.
- **Gypsum (3–4%)** is added during grinding to control setting time.

### 6.5 Storage
- Stored in **jute/gunny bags**.
- **10 bags** are stacked per pile.

## 7. Tests on Cement

### 7.1 Field Tests

**a) Colour Test**
- Cement should have **uniform grey colour with a light greenish shade**.

**b) Physical Test**
- Should feel **smooth** when rubbed between fingers.
- Sample should be **free from lumps** (lumps indicate moisture absorption).
- When made into paste, it should be **sticky**.
- When thrown in water, it should **sink after floating** for some time.
- When hand is inserted into cement bag, it should feel **cool**.

---

### 7.2 Laboratory Tests

#### Test 1: Fineness Test
Fineness is based on grain size. Two methods:

**a) Sieve Test:**
- Take 100 g of cement; place on IS Sieve No. 9 (90-micron sieve).
- Perform sieving for **15 minutes**.
- Residue should **not exceed 10%** (i.e., ≤ 10 g) for OPC.
- Exception: Rapid Hardening Cement (RHC) and Portland Pozzolana Cement (PPC) — residue should **not exceed 5%**.

**b) Air Permeability Test:**
- Expressed as **Specific Surface Area (SSA)** in cm²/g.
- SSA is proportional to fineness.
- Apparatus: **Blaine's Air Permeability Apparatus** (also: Nurse's apparatus, Wagner Turbidimeter).

| Cement Type | Minimum SSA |
|---|---|
| OPC | 2250 cm²/g |
| RHC, LHC | 3250 cm²/g |
| PPC | 3000 cm²/g |
| SSC (Super Sulphated Cement) | 4000 cm²/g |

---

#### Test 2: Standard Consistency Test

**Purpose:** To determine percentage of water needed to produce cement paste of standard consistency.

**Apparatus:** Vicat's Apparatus

**Vicat Apparatus Components:**
- **Mould:** Cylindrical; Height = 40 mm; Diameter = 60–80 mm
- **Plunger:** 10 mm diameter, 40–50 mm long
- **Square needle:** 1 mm² cross-section
- **Needle with annular collar:** 5 mm diameter

**Procedure:**
1. Take 500 g cement; add 23% water by weight.
2. **Time of gauging** (from water addition to mould filling): 3¾ to 4¼ minutes.
3. Lower plunger gently onto surface of mould and release quickly.
4. If penetration = **33–35 mm from top (or 5–7 mm from bottom)** → water content is correct.

**Result:** The moisture content at which standard consistency is achieved is called **'P'** (standard consistency). Normal range: **25–35%**; typical OPC: ~25%.

---

#### Test 3: Setting Time Test

**Purpose:** To determine if cement has deteriorated due to storage.  
**Apparatus:** Vicat's Apparatus  
**Sample:** 500 g cement + 0.85P water

**a) Initial Setting Time (IST):**
- Attachment: **Square needle (1 mm²)**
- Measured from: water addition → stage when needle **ceases to penetrate completely** (5 mm from bottom).
- For OPC: IST **≥ 30 minutes** (should not be less than 30 minutes).

**b) Final Setting Time (FST):**
- Attachment: **Needle with annular collar**
- When annular collar **fails to make impression** on mould but needle still shows.
- For OPC: FST **≤ 10 hours** (should not be more than 10 hours).

---

#### Test 4: Soundness Test

**Purpose:** To detect presence of uncombined (free) lime.  
Soundness issues arise from: **Lime, Magnesia, Sulphur**.

**a) Le Chatelier Test (for Lime):**
- Apparatus: **Le Chatelier apparatus**
- Mix: 100 g cement + 0.78P water; fill in mould.
- Cover top and bottom with glass plates; immerse in water at **27°–32°C for 24 hours**.
- Note displacement of split using indicator arm; then boil and note expansion.
- **Expansion ≤ 10 mm** (acceptable).
- Exception: High Alumina Cement and Super Sulphated Cement — **≤ 5 mm**.

**b) Autoclave Test (for Lime + Magnesia):**
- Apparatus: **Autoclave**
- Mould size: **25 × 25 × 250 mm**
- Steam pressure increased to **21 kg/cm²**, maintained for 3 hours.
- % expansion in each direction must **not exceed 0.8%**.

---

#### Test 5: Compressive Strength Test

- Cube size: **7.06 cm × 7.06 cm** (surface area = 50 cm²)
- Water needed = **P/4 + 3.5** (where P = standard consistency %)
- Strength requirements for OPC:

| Age | Minimum Strength |
|---|---|
| 3 days | 11.5 N/mm² (≈ 50% of 28-day strength) |
| 7 days | 17.5 N/mm² (≈ ⅔ of 28-day strength) |
| 28 days | 33 N/mm² |

---

#### Test 6: Tensile Strength Test

- **Briquette mould** is used.
- Briquette dimensions: Length = 76.2 mm, Thickness = 25.4 mm, Breadth = 44.45 mm
- Least cross-sectional area = **6.45 cm²**
- Mix ratio: **Cement : Sand = 1:3**
- Water = **P/4 + 2.5**
- Loading rate: **3.5 N/mm²**

| Age | Minimum Tensile Strength (OPC) |
|---|---|
| 3 days | 2.0 N/mm² |
| 7 days | 2.5 N/mm² |

> Tensile test gives **quicker indication of cement defects** than any other test.

---

#### Test 7: Specific Gravity Test

- Apparatus: **Le Chatelier flask**
- Flask filled with **kerosene (water-free) or naphtha** between 0 and 1 mL mark.
- **64 g of cement** is introduced; rise in volume is observed.
- **Specific gravity of cement (homogeneous particles) = 3.15**
- Bulk density of cement = **1440 kg/m³**

## 8. Types of Cement

### 1. Acid Resistant Cement
- Used in **chemical industries**.
- Aggregates: quartz, quartzite, etc.
- Does **not resist water** unless **linseed oil or ceresit** is added.

### 2. Blast Furnace Slag Cement
- Composition: **Blast furnace slag (60%) + clinker**.
- Slag is a waste product from pig iron manufacturing.
- Properties: **Cheap, durable, more resistant to weathering agents**.

### 3. Coloured Cement
- Obtained by adding **colouring pigment (5–10%)**.

| Pigment | Colour Produced |
|---|---|
| Chromium oxide | Green |
| Cobalt | Blue |
| Iron oxides | Greyish yellow, red, brown |
| Magnesium oxides | Brown or black |
| Aluminium oxides | White |

- **Snowcem** = coloured cement (trade name)
- **Colourcrete** = trade name of coloured cement
- **Snowcrete** = trade name of white cement

### 4. Expanding Cement
- Contains **sulpho-aluminate**.
- Uses: water-retaining structures, repair of damaged concrete surfaces.
- Neutralizes shrinkage of concrete → **eliminates cracks**.

### 5. High Alumina Cement (HAC)
- Main components: **limestone + 40% bauxite**.
- **Initial setting time: > 3.5 hours; Final setting time: ~5 hours**
- Gains **high ultimate strength within 72 hours (3 days)**.
- **Not used for mass concrete works**.

| Use | Reason |
|---|---|
| Marine construction, sewer infrastructure | Rapid hardening + high strength |
| Refractory lining, chimney, kiln lining | Withstands high temperature |
| Coastal areas concrete surfaces | Durability in aggressive environment |

### 6. Hydrophobic Cement
- Decreases **wetting ability** of cement.
- Formed by mixing admixtures: **acidol, oleic acid, oxidized petroleum, naphthene**, etc.
- Water-repellant layers protect cement from deterioration during storage.
- Used when cement must be stored in **damp conditions**.
- Properties: **frost and weather resistant**.

### 7. Low Heat Cement (LHC)
- Produced by:
  - Reducing C₃A to ~**5%** and C₃S.
  - Increasing C₂S to ~**46%** (strength gained slowly).
- **Slow rate of strength development**.
- Specific surface area = **3200 cm²/kg**
- **IST = 1 hour; FST = 10 hours**
- Used for: bulk concreting, mass concrete, retaining walls, **dam construction**.

### 8. Portland Pozzolana Cement (PPC)
- Pozzolana = siliceous + aluminous compound (**volcanic material with ~80% clay**).
- Made by adding **10–30% pozzolana** to OPC clinker.
- Specific surface area ≥ **300 m²/kg**
- Properties: more resistance to sulphates, less heat during setting, high shrinkage.
- Used for: mass concrete (dams), marine structures, sewage works.

### 9. Quick Setting Cement
- Contains **more aluminium sulphate** and **less gypsum**.
- Finely ground for easy/rapid setting.
- **IST = 5 minutes; FST = 30 minutes (up to 1 hour)**.
- Used for: underwater construction, grouting operations, repair works.

### 10. Rapid Hardening Cement (RHC)
- Also called **High Early Strength Cement**.
- Produced by: increasing C₃S, reducing C₂S, burning at high temperature, finer grinding.
- Fineness: ~**4500 cm²/g**
- More lime content.

**Strength Comparison:**

| RHC Age | Equivalent OPC Strength |
|---|---|
| 1 day | = OPC at 3 days |
| 3 days | = OPC at 7 days |

- Used for: prefabricated construction, road work, cold weather concreting, emergency repair.
- **Very costly**.

### 11. Extra Rapid Hardening Cement (ERHC)
- Produced by intergrinding RHC clinkers with **CaCl₂ (accelerator)** ≤ **2% by weight**.
- Strength comparison:

| Age | ERHC vs RHC |
|---|---|
| 1–2 days | ~20–25% higher than RHC |
| 7 days | ~10–15% higher than RHC |
| 90 days | Almost same as OPC |

- Applications: same as RHC; especially for **cold weather concreting**.

### 12. Sulphate Resisting Cement (SRC)
- OPC is highly susceptible to sulphate attack.
- Manufactured by **reducing C₃A and C₄AF**.
- Used for: foundation work, culverts, sewage treatment, marine structures, pipes in marshy areas, canal lining.

### 13. White Cement
- Free from iron, manganese, chromium (these cause colour).
- **Oil** is used for burning instead of coal.
- **Strength > OPC**.
- Used for: flooring, plastering of walls, ornamental works, paving tiles, marble work.

### 14. Modified Portland Cement
- Less heat of hydration.
- Used for: abutments, piers, retaining walls.

### 15. Air Entraining Cement
- Resists sulphate attack.
- Resists freezing and thawing.
- Less shrinkage and cracks.

## 9. Grades of Cement

Grades signify **28-day compressive strength** (in N/mm²). Higher grades introduced after 1987.

| Grade | 3-day Strength | 7-day Strength | 28-day Strength | Uses |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **33** | 16 N/mm² | 22 N/mm² | 33 N/mm² | General construction, mass concrete, plain plastering, precast blocks, flooring |
| **43** | 23 N/mm² | 33 N/mm² | 43 N/mm² | Medium structures, precast blocks, plastering, flooring, asbestos products (sheets, pipes), RCC |
| **53** | 27 N/mm² | 37 N/mm² | 53 N/mm² | Strong structures — bridges, highways, high-rise buildings, concrete roads, precast pavement blocks |
