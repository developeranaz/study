## 1. CLASSIFICATION OF STONES

### Physical Classification (3 types)

| Type | Formation | Examples | Exception |
|------|-----------|----------|-----------|
| **Stratified** | Layer by layer; can be split easily | Sedimentary rocks — Limestone, Shale, Slate | Slate (metamorphic) |
| **Unstratified** | No definite layers; crystalline/granular/compact | Igneous rocks — Granite, Basalt, Trap | Marble (metamorphic) |
| **Foliated** | Splits in one definite direction only | Metamorphic rocks — Gneiss | Marble, Quartzite |

### Chemical Classification

| Type | Dominant Constituent | Examples |
|------|----------------------|----------|
| **Siliceous** | Silica | Gneiss, Syenite, Quartzite |
| **Argillaceous** | Clay / Alumina | Slates, Laterites, Schist, Kaolin |
| **Calcareous** | Calcium carbonate | Limestone, Dolomite, Marble |

### Key Rock Examples
- **Granite** = Igneous + Unstratified + Siliceous
- **Marble** = Metamorphic + Unstratified + Calcareous

## 2. MINERALS

### Monomineralic vs Polymineralic
- **Monomineralic** — one mineral only → Quartzite, Magnesite
- **Polymineralic** — several minerals → Basalt, Granite
  - Granite = Feldspar + Mica + Quartz
  - Basalt = Plagioclase + Pyroxene

| **Plagioclase** | Series of feldspar |

| **Quartz** | Crystalline silica; colourless; density 2.65 g/cm³ |

## 5. PROPERTIES OF MINERALS

1. **Cleavage** — Ability to split along parallel planes
2. **Streak** — Colour of mineral in powdered form
3. **Lustre** — Shiny appearance in reflected light
4. **Natural Bed** — Distinct plane of division; stone can be split along it

- **Arches** → radial
- **Cornices, string courses** → vertical
- **Walls** → horizontal

## 6. TESTS FOR STONES

| Test | Purpose | Key Details |
|------|---------|-------------|
| **Acid Test** | Detect calcium carbonate | Place in 1% HCl for 7 days; used for sandstone, NOT limestone |
| **Attrition Test** | Rate of wear | Deval's machine; 60mm stone; 30 RPM; 5 hrs; wear ≤ 3% |
| **Crushing Test** | Compressive strength | 40×40×40mm cube; soak 3 days; load rate 13.7 N/mm²; CS = max load/bearing area |
| **Crystallization Test (IS 1186)** | Durability/weathering | 4 cubes (40mm); immerse 2 hrs in 14% Na₂SO₄ |
| **Freezing & Thawing Test** | Climatic deformation | Freeze at −12°C for 24 hrs |
| **Hardness Test** | Hardness | Dorry's machine; disc at 28 RPM; CoH = 20 − (loss in weight/3) |
| **Impact Test** | Toughness | 2 kg steel hammer; falls axially |
| **Microscopic Test** | Grain size, texture, minerals | — |
| **Smith's Test** | Soluble matter / muddy appearance | — |
| **Water Absorption Test** | Water absorption % | — |
| **Brads Test** | Frost resistance | — |
| **Fire Resistant Test** | CaCO₃ presence | — |

### Quality Standards for Good Stones

| Property | Standard |
|----------|----------|
| Coefficient of Hardness | > 17 (good for roads); 14–17 medium; < 14 poor |
| Toughness Index (Impact) | ≥ 13; < 13 not tough; 13–19 moderate |
| Attrition Wear | ≤ 2% excellent; = 3% tolerable; > 3% not satisfactory |
| Water Absorption (roads) | ≤ 0.6% or ≤ 5%; > 10% → stone rejected |
| Specific Gravity | > 2.7 (Marble: 2.4–2.7; Granite: 2.6–2.7) |
| Crushing Strength | > 100 N/mm² (Granite: 75–127; Limestone: 54; Sandstone: 64) |
| Quarry Sap | Fresh stones contain moisture; seasoning required 6–12 months |

## 7. QUARRYING

**Definition:** Process of extracting stones from rocks.

### Methods

#### 1. Hand Tools
- **(a) Digging/Excavation** — axes, hammers, jumpers, chisels; for soft stones in block form
- **(b) Heating** — top surface heated (wood fire); stones separate due to unequal expansion
- **(c) Wedging** — steel wedges (plug & feather) driven into cracks using hammer
  - Plug = conical wedge (centre)
  - Feather = flat wedge with curved end at top
  - Used in stratified rocks with cracks/fissures

#### 2. Channeling Machines
- Grooves driven by steam / compressed air / electric machines
- Groove: Length = 24m, Depth = 2.4–3.7m, Width = 50–75mm

#### 3. Blasting
- Explosives convert rock into small pieces
- Small pieces used: railways, concrete aggregate, road metal

### Steps for Blasting
1. **Boring** — drill hole
2. **Cleaning** — remove dust
3. **Charging** — load explosive
4. **Tamping** — fill back hole
5. **Firing** — ignite

**Line of Least Resistance (LLR):** Shortest distance between charge centre and nearest rock face

**Quantity of explosives = (LLR in m)² × 1.5**

### Blasting Tools

| Tool | Use |
|------|-----|
| Dipper | Drill hole to required depth |
| Jumper | Drill vertical blast holes |
| Priming Needle | Thin copper rod; insert fuse |
| Scraping Spoon | Clean bore hole; iron rod |
| Tamping Bar | Ram material back into hole; brass rod; 10–15mm dia |

### Explosives Comparison

| Property | Blasting Powder (Gunpowder) | Dynamite |
|----------|----------------------------|----------|
| Composition | Charcoal(15%) + KNO₃(75%) + Sulphur(10%) | Sandy earth(25%) + Nitroglycerin(75%) |
| Cost | Cheap | 5× costlier |
| Strength | Weak | 6× stronger |
| Volume loosened per 1N | 0.4 m³ | 0.6 m³ |
| Best for | Large block quarrying; lifting | Tunneling; damp/underwater |

### Other Explosives

| Explosive | Composition | Special Feature |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|
| **Blasting Gelatin** | Nitroglycerin(93%) + Gun cotton(7%) | 50% more powerful than dynamite; jelly form |
| **Cordite** | Nitroglycerin + Nitrocellulose | No smoke; underwater |
| **Gelignite** | Blasting gelatin(65%) + Absorbing powder(35%) | More convenient than dynamite; underwater |
| **Gun Cotton** | Cotton + nitric acid + sulphuric acid | As strong as dynamite; low shattering; store damp; for demolitions |
| **Liquid Oxygen** | Liquid oxygen | Cheaper; large scale |
| **Rock-a-Rock** | KClO₃(79%) + Nitrobenzol(21%) | Best for underwater/damp |
| **Lithofracteur** | — | Tunnels |

### Detonator
- Copper cylinder: 6mm dia × 25mm length
- Closed one end; fuse at other
- Contains 6–9 grains of fulminate of mercury
- Used mainly with dynamite

### Fuse
- Cotton rope, tar coated
- Burns at 10mm/second

## TIMBER BASICS

- Derived from Old English: *Timbrian*
- Wood for engineering purposes = **Timber**
- Modulus of Elasticity: **1×10⁴ to 1.5×10⁴ N/mm²**

## Properties

### 1. Weight
- Hygroscopic in nature
- Weight determined at **12% moisture content**
- Specific gravity = 1.54
- Density = 800–900 kg/m³

### 2. Strength
- Along grain > Across grain
- Strength along grain → columns, problems
- Strength across grain → railway sleepers
- Sawing always done **parallel to grain**

### 3. Fire Resistance

**Method 1 — Sir Abell's Process (3 steps):**
1. Diluted sodium silicate
2. Slaked lime
3. Concentrated sodium silicate

**Method 2 — Chemical treatment:**
- Borax solution
- Ammonium sulphate solution
- Sodium arsenite

| Type | Definition | Examples |
|------|-----------|---------|
| **Refractory** | Non-resinous; does not catch fire easily | Sal, Teak |
| **Non-Refractory** | Resinous; catches fire easily | Chir, Deodar, Fir |

## Classification of Timber

### Based on Position (IS 399-1963)
1. **Standing** — timber in living tree
2. **Rough** — timber after felling
3. **Converted** — sawn/cut to commercial sizes

### Based on Modulus of Elasticity
| Group | E Value |
|-------|---------|
| A | > 12.5 kN/mm² |
| B | 9.8–12.8 kN/mm² |
| C | 5.6–9.8 kN/mm² |

## 1. BRICKS — BASICS

- **Definition:** Clay moulded in rectangular blocks, dried and burned → bricks
- **IS Code (bricks):** 1077
- **IS Code (brick masonry):** 2212
- **Unit weight of brick masonry:** 2000 kg/m³

### Brick Sizes

| Type | Size |
|------|------|
| Standard / Modular | 19 × 9 × 9 cm or 19 × 9 × 4 cm |
| Nominal (incl. mortar) | 20 × 10 × 10 cm |
| Traditional / Non-modular | 23 × 11 × 7 cm (or 23 × 11 × 7.6 cm) |

### Frog
- Depression at top surface of brick
- Acts as key between mortar and brick
- Size: 10 × 4 × 1 cm
- Made by pallet boards + wooden/moulding/stock board

---

## 2. INGREDIENTS OF GOOD BRICK EARTH

| Ingredient | % | Role |
|-----------|---|------|
| **Silica** | 50–60% | Strength, durability, uniform shape; prevents warping/cracking/shrinking |
| **Alumina** | 20–30% | Chief constituent; absorbs water; imparts plasticity |
| **Lime** | < 5% | Flux (fuses silica at kiln temp); prevents shrinkage |
| **Iron Oxide** | 5–6% | Red colour; hardness; acts as flux |
| **Magnesia** | 1–4% | Yellow tint; decreases shrinkage |
| **Alkalies, CO₂** | Very small | — |
| **Sulphur trioxide** | Very small | — |

### Excess Effects
- **Excess Silica** → brittle bricks
- **Excess Alumina** → shrinkage + warping
- **Excess Lime** → brick melts, loses shape
- **Excess Iron Oxide** → dark blue/black colour
- **Excess Magnesia** → decay of bricks

### Harmful Ingredients

| Ingredient | Harmful Effect |
|-----------|----------------|
| **Lime** | Brick melts, shape loss |
| **Iron Pyrites (FeS₂)** | Crystallization → disintegration → brick splits (oxidation) |
| **Alkalies (Soda/Potash)** | **Efflorescence** — white/grey powder deposits when moisture evaporates |
| **Pebbles/Gravels** | Uneven mixing; brick becomes porous |
| **Vegetation/Organic matter** | Brick becomes porous; cracks; **bloating** (black mass on surface) |
| **Sulphur** | Spongy, swollen structure during burning |

> **Efflorescence:** ≤ 10% deposit = Slight efflorescence
> Also caused by gypsum and pyrites.

### Characteristics of Good Brick
- Uniform bright colour; sharp edges; no cracks
- Clear metallic ringing sound when struck
- Fracture: bright, homogeneous, compact
- Hard — no scratch with fingernail
- Does not break when dropped from 1m height
- Crushing strength ≥ 5.5 N/mm²
- No white salt deposits after 24h water soak
- Uniform shape and size

---

## 3. MANUFACTURING OF BRICKS

### Process Overview
1. Preparation of clay
2. Moulding
3. Drying
4. Burning

### I. Preparation of Clay (6 steps)

| Step | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Unsoiling** | Remove top 20cm (impurities) |
| **Digging** | Spread clay 60–120cm heaps |
| **Cleaning** | Remove stones, pebbles, vegetation; powder form |
| **Weathering** | Expose to atmosphere (monsoon); uniform moisture + consistency |
| **Blending** | Harmonious mixing; good ingredients added, harmful removed |
| **Tempering** | Water added; homogeneous mix; proper hardness |

**Tempering methods:**
- Under feet of men/cattle (kneading)
- **Pug Mill** (pugging): Conical shape; Top dia 1m, Bottom dia 800mm, Height 2m

### II. Moulding

- Mould size = **8–12% larger** than standard brick size (to account for shrinkage)

#### Hand Moulding
- Small scale; rectangular boxes open top and bottom
- **Ground moulded** — on ground level
- **Table moulded** — on 2m×1m table; 750 bricks in 8 hours

**Types:**
1. **Sand moulding** — white sand on mould sides → proper shape ✓
2. **Slop moulding** — mould dipped in water → proper shape not always obtained

#### Machine Moulding
1. **Plastic clay machine** → Wire cut bricks (clay in plastic state)
2. **Dry clay machine** → Pressed/hard bricks (no drying required)

### III. Drying

| Method | Time | Details |
|--------|------|---------|
| Natural / Hack drying | 3–10 days | Stacked 6–8 tiers; reduce to ~2% moisture; protected from sun/rain |
| Artificial drying | 1–3 days | Tunnel dryer; temp < 120°C |

### IV. Burning

- Temp range: **350–1100°C**
- > 1100°C → shape loss
- **Overburnt** → brittle, breaks easily
- **Underburnt** → soft, cannot carry load

#### Kilns

| Kiln | Type | Key Points |
|------|------|-----------|
| **Updraught intermittent** | Intermittent | — |
| **Downdraught intermittent** | Intermittent | More efficient |
| **Hoffman's Kiln (Flame Kiln)** | Continuous | Worldwide used; 365 days/year; 12 chambers; 25,000 bricks/day; overground; can work in rain |
| **Bull's Trench Kiln** | Continuous | Most used in India; oval plan; 50–100m long; 1.5–2.5m deep; 8–12 sections; cannot work in monsoon; underground/semi-underground |
| **Tunnel Kiln** | Continuous | Straight or circular; bricks on trolleys; stationary fire; large scale; costly |

**Bull's Trench Working Sequence:**
1. Loading → 2. Preheating (350–600°C) → 3. Burning (1100°C) → 4. Cooling → 5. Unloading

## 4. TESTS ON BRICKS

| Test | Key Detail | Standard |
|------|-----------|---------|
| **Absorption Test** | Dry at 110–115°C; immerse 16–24h; weigh | ≤ 20% dry weight |
| **Crushing Strength** | CTM machine | Min 3.5 N/mm² (IS 1077); Grade A: 7–14; Grade AA: >14; Building min: 5.5 N/mm² |
| **Hardness** | Scratch with fingernail | No impression = sufficient hardness |
| **Soluble Salts** | Brick in distilled water; absorb + evaporate | No grey/white deposits = good quality |
| **Size Test** | 20 bricks arranged in line | Length: 3800 ± 120mm; Width: 1800 ± 60mm |
| **Soundness** | Strike bricks against each other | Clear metallic ring; no break |
| **Structure** | Visual inspection | Homogeneous, compact, no defects |

## 7. CLASSIFICATION OF BRICKS (by quality)
