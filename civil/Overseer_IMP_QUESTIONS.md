## 1. STONES

### Rock Classification

| Basis | Types | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Origin** | Igneous, Sedimentary, Metamorphic | Granite, Sandstone, Marble |
| **Chemical** | Siliceous, Calcareous, Argillaceous | Quartzite, Marble, Shale |
| **Structure** | Stratified, Unstratified, Foliated | Limestone, Granite, Slate |

### Types in Detail

**Igneous Rocks**
- Formed by solidification of molten mass (magma) below or above earth's surface.
- Have **crystalline structure**.
- Example: **Granite** (plutonic/intrusive igneous rock).

**Sedimentary Rocks**
- Formed by consolidation of particles of pre-existing rocks under weathering agencies.
- Also called **aqueous/stratified rocks**.
- Classification: Siliceous, Calcareous, Argillaceous OR Residual, Chemical, Organic.
- Example: **Sandstone** (siliceous sedimentary rock).
- **Sandstone** under metamorphic action → **Quartzite**.

**Metamorphic Rocks**
- Formed by alteration of original structure under heat and excessive pressure.
- Examples: **Slate** (from Shale), **Quartzite** (from Sandstone), **Marble** (from Limestone).
- Geologically, slate and marble are metamorphic rocks.
- Both slate and quartzite are metamorphic rocks.

**Special Rocks**

| Rock | Type | Sub-type |
|---|---|---|
| Granite | Igneous | Plutonic |
| Sandstone | Sedimentary | Siliceous |
| Slate | Metamorphic | Argillaceous |
| Quartzite | Metamorphic | Siliceous |
| Marble | Metamorphic | Calcareous |
| Laterite | Sedimentary | Argillaceous |
| Limestone | Sedimentary | Calcareous |

> **Note:** Marble is an **unstratified** rock.

### Properties of Good Building Stone

| Property | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Moisture absorption | ≤ **1%** |
| Specific gravity | **2.4 – 2.8** |
| Crushing strength | **> 100 MPa** |
| Hardness (in-situ test) | Schmidt hammer test |

### Stone Tools & Processes

| Tool/Process | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Spalling hammer** | Rough dressing of stones |
| **Cross cut saw** | Cutting hard stones |
| **Jumper** | Quarrying of stones |
| **Dressing** | Providing smooth/regular face to stones |
| **Quarrying** | Extraction of stones from natural bed rock |

## 2. BRICKS

### Standard Sizes

| Type | Size (L × W × H) |
|---|---|
| Standard brick (with mortar — nominal) | **20 × 10 × 10 cm** |
| Standard brick (actual/modular) | **19 × 9 × 9 cm** |
| Traditional brick | **18 × 8 × 4.5 cm** (commonly adopted) |

- Bricks required per cubic meter of brickwork: **500**

### Composition of Good Brick Earth

| Constituent | % Range | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Silica** | 50–60% | Durability; excess → brittle and weak |
| **Alumina** | 20–30% | Plasticity; excess → crack and warp on drying |
| **Lime** | Small amount | Binding; excess → causes shape loss |
| **Iron oxide** | Small amount | Red colour; |
| **Magnesia** | Trace | — |

> **Iron pyrites** is NOT a constituent of good brick earth.

### Manufacturing of Bricks — Steps
1. **Preparation of clay** — digging, weathering, blending
2. **Tempering (Pugging)** — mixing clay with water in a **pug mill**
3. **Moulding** — hand moulding (superior for facing) or machine moulding
4. **Drying** — in air for **3 to 8 days**, not in direct sun
5. **Burning** — at **900°C to 1200°C**

> Hand-made bricks are **superior** to machine-made for facing purposes.

### Burning Methods

| Method | Advantage |
|---|---|
| **Clamp** | Less initial cost |
| **Kiln** | Less time; more first-class bricks; for large quantities |

### Brick Classes

| Class | Water absorption (24 hrs) | Min. Crushing Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **First class** | ≤ 15% | 140 kg/m² (≈ 10 N/mm²) |

- **Overburnt bricks (Jhama)** → used for road soling
- **Refractory bricks** → used for furnace lining
- **Efflorescence** → whitish deposit due to soluble salts in bricks

### Brick Terminology

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Frog** | Depression at top of brick; placed on **top face** in construction |
| **Voussoir** | Wedge-shaped brick used in arches |
| **Half bat** | Half the width of a full brick |
| **Terracotta** | Clay product; essentially a clay-burnt product |
| **Refractories** | Substances that resist high temperature |

## 4. CEMENT

### Composition of Portland Cement

| Ingredient | % | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Lime (CaO)** | ~63% (max ingredient) | Strength; excess → expansion and disintegration |
| **Silica (SiO₂)** | ~22% | Strength; excess → slow setting |
| **Alumina (Al₂O₃)** | ~6% | Quick setting; excess → reduces strength |
| **Iron oxide** | ~3% | Colour (grey) |
| **MgO** | ~2% (min ingredient) | — |

- Manufacture proportions: **Lime 63%, Silica 22%, Others 15%**
- **Calcareous materials**: Limestone, Chalk, Marine shells.
- **Argillaceous materials**: Major content = **Alumina**.
- **Efflorescence** in cement → excess **Alkalis**.
- **Colour** in cement → **Iron oxide**.

### Manufacturing Process
1. Raw materials crushed and mixed.
2. Burnt in **Rotary kiln** → forms **Clinker** (small lumps formed at **1500°C**).
3. **Gypsum** (3–5%) added to retard initial setting.
4. Final grinding → sieved through IS sieve No. **9 (90 µm)**; residue ≤ 5%.

### Bogue's Compounds of Cement

| Compound | Formula | Key Property |
|---|---|---|
| **Tricalcium silicate (C₃S)** | 3CaO·SiO₂ | Early strength; high heat of hydration |
| **Dicalcium silicate (C₂S)** | 2CaO·SiO₂ | Late strength (14–28 days); low heat |
| **Tricalcium aluminate (C₃A)** | 3CaO·Al₂O₃ | Sets first; max heat of hydration |
| **Tetracalcium aluminoferrite (C₄AF)** | 4CaO·Al₂O₃·Fe₂O₃ | Least heat; contributes to colour |

- **C₃A reacts first** with water → initial setting.
- **C₃A** gives **maximum heat of hydration**.
- High C₃S + Low C₂S → Rapid hardening + High early strength.
- Slow setting cement → higher % of **C₂S**.
- Hydration = **exothermic** reaction.

### Types of Cement

| Type | Use |
|---|---|
| **Low heat cement** | Mass concrete (dams); use C₂S more, C₃S less |
| **Rapid hardening cement** | Early strength; higher C₃S |
| **Blast furnace slag cement** | Mass concrete dam construction |
| **White cement** | Least % of **Iron oxide** |
| **Hydrophobic cement** | Contains **Oleic acid** |
| **High alumina cement** | Initial setting time = **30 minutes** |
| **Quick setting cement** | Contains **Aluminium sulphate**; IST = 5 min, FST = 30 min |
| **Portland pozzolana cement (PPC)** | Mass concrete; reduces heat |

### Cement Testing

| Test | Apparatus | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Consistency** | Vicat's apparatus (10 mm plunger) | Normal consistency (~30%) |
| **Setting time** | Vicat's apparatus | IST + FST |
| **Soundness** | Le-Chatelier apparatus | Free lime/magnesia expansion |
| **Fineness** | Air permeability test / IS sieve No.9 | Degree of grinding |
| **Compressive strength** | 70.7 mm cube (cement:sand = 1:3) | Tested at 3, 7, 28 days |

- Water for setting time test = **0.85P** (P = standard consistency)
- Vicat plunger diameter: **10 mm**
- Expansion should not exceed: **10 mm** for any Portland cement
- Specific surface area of good OPC: ≥ **2250 sq cm/gm**
- Specific gravity of OPC: **3.15**

### IS Codes — Cement Grades

| Grade | 28-day Mean Compressive Strength |
|---|---|
| 33 grade | 33 MPa |
| 43 grade | > **43 MPa** (mean > 43.5 MPa per IS) |
| 53 grade | 53 MPa |

- IS: **23 grade** is NOT available in Indian market.
- OPC IS sieve residue ≤ **5%** on 90 µm sieve.
- IS code specifies OPC 3-day strength ≥ **16 MPa**.

### Physical Properties

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Weight per bag | **50 kg** |
| Volume per bag | **0.0345 m³** |
| Bulk density | **1400–1700 kg/m³** |
| Sieve number for fineness | IS Sieve No. **9** (90 µm) |

## 5. LIME

### Types of Lime

| Type | Description | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Fat lime / Rich lime** | Almost pure CaO; slakes rapidly with heat; high plasticity | Whitewashing |
| **Lean / Poor lime** | Impure; doesn't slake well | — |
| **Hydraulic lime** | Has silica/alumina; sets under water | Mortar, wet conditions |
| **Quick lime** | CaO (before slaking) | — |
| **Slaked / Hydrated lime** | Ca(OH)₂ | Plastering, whitewash |

- Chemical formula: Quick lime = **CaO**; Slaked lime = **Ca(OH)₂**; Limestone = **CaCO₃**
- Lime occurs naturally not in free state; made from **limestone (CaCO₃) or dolomite**.

### Manufacturing Process
1. **Calcination** — burning limestone to 900°C; CO₂ released → CaO formed.
2. **Slaking** — adding water to CaO → Ca(OH)₂ (hydrated lime).

### Hydraulic Lime
- Main constituent: **Silica** (imparts hydraulicity).
- **Eminently hydraulic lime**: silica + alumina + iron oxide = **25–30%**.
- Obtained by burning **Kankar**.
- Suitable for: mortar (not whitewashing).
- Used for underwater/damp conditions: **Eminently hydraulic lime**.

### IS 712 Classification

| Class | Type |
|---|---|
| Class A | Eminently hydraulic lime; IST = **15 minutes** |
| Class B | Semi-hydraulic / Fat lime |
| Class C | Fat lime |
| Class D | Dolomitic lime |

> **Fat lime** → suitable for whitewashing.  
> **Hydraulic lime** → suitable for making mortar, underwater work.

## 6. AGGREGATES

### Classification

| Basis | Types |
|---|---|
| **Size** | Fine (< 4.75 mm), Coarse (> 4.75 mm) |
| **Origin** | Natural, Artificial |
| **Density** | Normal, Lightweight, Heavyweight |

- Fine aggregate maximum particle size: **4.75 mm**
- Total volume of aggregates in concrete: **70–80%**
- Aggregates in concrete = **inert ingredient / filler**

### Shape of Aggregates

| Shape | Effect on Concrete |
|---|---|
| **Angular** | Maximum strength; decreases workability |
| **Rounded** | Maximum workability; less strength |
| **Flaky** | Decreases workability; poor strength |
| **Elongated** | Decreases workability |

- **Flaky aggregate**: least dimension < **0.6 times** mean dimension.
- **Elongated aggregate**: length > **1.8 times** mean dimension.
- Most desirable for lab mix design: **Saturated Surface Dry (SSD)** aggregates.

### Aggregate Tests

| Test | What it Measures | Sample Size |
|---|---|---|
| **Crushing test** | Crushing strength | Passing 12.5 mm, retained on 10 mm sieve |
| **Impact test** | Toughness | — |
| **Los Angeles abrasion test** | Abrasion resistance | — |
| **Soundness test** | Resistance to weathering | — |

- **Aggregate Impact Value (AIV)** limits:
  - Building concrete < **45%**
  - Road pavement < **30%**
  - Runway < **30%**

### Fineness Modulus (FM)

| Aggregate | FM Range |
|---|---|
| Fine aggregate | **2.0 – 3.5** |
| Coarse aggregate | **3.5 – 5.0** |
| All-in aggregate | **5.0 – 7.0** |

- FM is proportional to **grading** of aggregates.
- **Bulking of sand** is maximum at moisture content of **5%** (approximately 4–5%).
- **Gap graded**: a few sizes/fractions missing.
- Coarsest sand in IS: 383 → **Zone I**.

### Max Size of Aggregate
- For cement concrete beam: **20 mm**

## 7. WATER IN CONCRETE

- Water-cement (w/c) ratio = **Weight of water / Weight of cement**.
- Relationship between w/c ratio and concrete strength established by **Duff Abrams**.
- Lower w/c ratio → increased density, strength, impermeability (all improve).
- **pH of water** for mixing/curing: should **not be less than 6**.
- Permissible organic matter in water: **200 mg/l**.
- Sea water in concrete → loss of strength + corrosion of reinforcement + dampness + efflorescence (all).
- Minimum w/c ratio for workable concrete: **0.4**.

## 8. ADMIXTURES

### Definition
Materials added to concrete before or after mixing (other than cement, aggregate, water) to modify properties.

### Types

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Chemical** | Plasticizers, Superplasticizers, Accelerators, Retarders, Air-entraining agents |
| **Mineral** | Fly ash, Silica fume, Blast Furnace Slag, Pozzolans |

> **Set retarders** belong to chemical admixtures, **NOT** mineral admixtures.  
> **Pozzolanic materials** are mineral admixtures, not chemical.

### Key Admixtures

| Admixture | Function |
|---|---|
| **Plasticizers** | Reduce water needed for same workability |
| **Superplasticizers** | Greater water reduction than plasticizers |
| **Accelerators (CaCl₂)** | Early setting/hardening; offset cold weather retardation |
| **Retarders** | Delay setting; offset hot weather acceleration; e.g. Gypsum, Sugar, Calcium fluosilicate |
| **Sugar** | Retards setting of concrete |
| **Gypsum** | Acts as a **retarder** (in cement manufacture; retards C₃A) |
| **CaCl₂** | Acts as **accelerator** |

## 9. CONCRETE — MIX & PRODUCTION

### Nominal Mix Ratios (Cement : Sand : Aggregate)

| Grade | Mix Ratio |
|---|---|
| **M10** | 1 : 3 : 6 |
| **M15** | 1 : 2 : 4 |
| **M20** | 1 : 1.5 : 3 |
| **M25** | 1 : 1 : 2 |

- General building construction: **M15 (1:2:4)** → nominal ratio.
- **Characteristic strength** measured at **28 days**.
- M20 grade = concrete cube strength at 28 days = **20 N/mm²** (not kg/cm²).
- **Plain concrete** strong in compression; **Steel** added for tension.

### Density

| Type | Density (kg/m³) |
|---|---|
| PCC | **2200–2300** |
| RCC | **2400–2500** |

### Batching
- **Batching** = measurement of materials.
- **Volume batching** avoided due to **bulking of sand**.
- Hand mixing → add **10% extra cement**.

### Compaction
- Maximum height for placing concrete to prevent segregation: **150 cm**.
- **Needle vibrator** → most effective; used in most situations.
- **Table vibrator** → used for compacting cube specimens.
- Inadequate compaction → **honey-combing**.

### Transporting
- For dam construction: **Bucket and ropeway**.

### Curing
- Curing = keeping concrete moist to **promote hydration** and **eliminate shrinkage**.
- Formwork from sides of beams removed after **4 days**.

### Lightweight Concrete
Produced by:
- Using lightweight aggregate
- Introducing large voids
- Omitting fine aggregates

## 10. CONCRETE PROPERTIES & TESTING

### Workability

**Definition:** Ease with which concrete can be mixed, transported, and placed.  
**Most influenced by:** Water content.

| Test | Unit | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Slump test** | mm | Frustum of cone mould; Dia: bottom 20 cm, top 10 cm, height 30 cm |
| **Compaction factor test** | (dimensionless ratio) | — |
| **Vee-Bee consistometer test** | Seconds | — |

**Slump values and workability:**

| Slump (mm) | Workability |
|---|---|
| 0 – 25 | Very low |
| 25 – 50 | Low |
| 50 – 75 | Medium |
| 75 – 100 | High |
| > 100 | Very High |

- High workability needed for **heavily reinforced sections**.
- Compaction factor for high workability: **0.95**.
- Compaction factor of 0.88 → **Medium** workability.
- Vee-Bee time for low workability: **6–12 seconds**.

**To increase workability:** Increase cement content OR use rounded aggregates OR increase w/c ratio.

### Segregation & Bleeding

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Segregation** | Separation of coarse aggregates from concrete mix; caused by wetter mix / coarser grading |
| **Bleeding** | Separation of water/water-cement from freshly mixed concrete |
| **Honey-combing** | Result of segregation; voids/porous layers in hardened concrete |

### Compressive Strength Testing

- Standard cubes: **150 mm × 150 mm × 150 mm**.
- Test ages: **3, 7, 28 days**.
- Number of cube specimens per test: **3**.
- Cube strength = **1.25 × cylinder strength**.
- Flexural strength specimen: **15 × 15 × 75 cm** prism.

### Tensile Strength
Determined by:
- Direct tension test
- Split tension test (Brazilian test)
- Flexural strength / Modulus of rupture

## 11. TIMBER

### Tree Classification
- **Exogenous trees** (grow outward): Teak, Shisham, Sal — timber trees.
- **Endogenous trees** (grow inward): **Coconut**, Bamboo — NOT exogenous.

### Structure of a Tree (Cross-Section, outer to inner)

| Layer | Description |
|---|---|
| **Bark** | Outermost protective layer |
| **Cambium layer** | Between inner bark and sapwood; zone of growth |
| **Sapwood** | Between heartwood and cambium; newer wood |
| **Heartwood** | Innermost; darker; stronger; older rings |
| **Pith** | Central core of the tree |
| **Medullary rays** | Fibres extending from pith outwards |
| **Annular rings** | Indicate **age** of tree |

### Seasoning
- **Purpose:** Remove moisture from timber.
- Well-seasoned timber moisture content: **10–12%**.
- Natural seasoning time: **about 30 days**.

### Sawing Methods — Wastage

| Method | Wastage |
|---|---|
| **Quarter sawing / Radial sawing** | Least wastage |
| **Ordinary / Tangential sawing** | **Maximum wastage** |

### Defects in Timber

| Defect | Cause |
|---|---|
| **Knot** | Branch stub enclosed in growing wood |
| **Cup shake** | Defect in timber (separation along annual ring) |
| **Heart shake** | Radial splits, wider outside, narrower towards pith = **Star shakes** |
| **Blue stain** | Fungal defect |
| **Dry rot** | Reduces timber to **powder** |
| **Chip mark** | Tool mark; NOT a fungal defect |
| **Upsets** | Compression failure |

- Timber with maximum resistance against white ants: **Teak**.
- **ASCU** = Preservative for timber (Arsenic, Copper, Chrome compounds).

### Timber Products

| Product | Description |
|---|---|
| **Plywood** | Assembled from veneers glued at **90° (grains perpendicular)**; pressure = 100–150 N/cm² |
| **Plank** | Thickness < 5 cm, width > 12 cm |
| **Batten** | Thickness < 5 cm, width < 12 cm |
| **Log** | Trunk of tree after branches removed |
| **Baulk** | Roughly squared timber |

- Plywood specified by: **Thickness**.
- Plywood made from **common timber**.
- Plywood has **same tensile strength in all directions** (due to cross-grain layering).
- Timber: maximum strength **parallel to grain**.

### Timber Life
- First class timber: average life > **10 years**.

## 12. FOUNDATION (SUBSTRUCTURE)

| Type | Definition |
|---|---|

## 17. MORTAR

- **Mortar** = Binding material + Fine aggregate + **Water**.
- Sand in lime mortar → **improve strength**.
- Lime mortar prepared with **Hydraulic lime** (not quick lime/fat lime alone).
- **Gauged mortar** = mortar prepared with wood powder/raw dust + sand + lime (actually: gauged = cement added to lime mortar; light mortar with wood powder = **lightweight mortar**).

### Mortar Proportions for Common Work

| Work | Cement:Sand Ratio |
|---|---|
| Pointing work | **1:2** |
| Plastering work | **1:3** to **1:6** (1:3 for interior; 1:6 for external) |

## 19. STAIRS

### Types of Stairs

| Type | Turn |
|---|---|
| **Straight stair** | No turn |
| **Quarter turn stair** | 90° turn |
| **Dog-legged stair** | **Half turn (180°)** — most common residential type |

### Stair Dimensions

| Dimension | Residential | Public Buildings |
|---|---|---|
| Width of stair | **80–90 cm** | Min. **1.0 m** |

| Headroom | — | Min. **2.0 m** (some codes: 2.1–2.2 m) |

- **Nosing** = projection on step of stair.
- **Landing** = used for changing direction (rest platform).
- **Flight** = continuous series of steps between landings.
