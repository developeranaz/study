### Stopping Sight Distance (SSD) Problems

**Q1: What is the braking distance to stop a vehicle moving at 54 km/h, given coefficient of friction = 0.3 and g = 10 m/s²?**

- (A) 37.5 m
- (B) 45.0 m
- (C) 27.0 m
- (D) 37.5 m

✅ **Answer: D — 37.5 m**

📝 **Explanation:** Two equivalent formulas apply:
- If velocity is in km/h: BD = V² / (254f)
- If velocity is in m/s: BD = v² / (2gf)

Convert 54 km/h → 15 m/s. Then BD = (15)² / (2 × 10 × 0.3) = 225 / 6 = **37.5 m**

**Q2: A state highway road is to be constructed with a carriageway width of 7 m and a camber of 1 in 50. Find the crown height.**

✅ **Answer: D — 70 mm**

📝 **Explanation:** Crown Height = (W/2) × (1/N), where W = carriageway width and N = camber ratio.

Crown Height = (7/2) × (1/50) = 3.5 / 50 = 0.07 m = **70 mm**

💡 **Note:** Always verify units in the options — the answer in metres (0.07 m) must be converted to mm (70 mm) to match the option.

**Q3: When brake efficiency increases, what happens to Stopping Sight Distance (SSD)?**

- (A) Decreases
- (B) Increases
- (C) Remains same
- (D) First increases then decreases

✅ **Answer: A — Decreases**

📝 **Explanation:** SSD = vt + v² / (2g·f·η), where η = brake efficiency. Since η appears in the denominator of the braking distance term, increasing η reduces the braking distance, and hence reduces the total SSD.

💡 **Note:** When brake efficiency is given as a percentage, convert it to decimal form before substituting (e.g., 60% → 0.6).

**Q4: Calculate the SSD for a design speed of 60 km/h on a two-way traffic single-lane road. Reaction time = 2.5 s, f = 0.36, g = 9.8 m/s².**

✅ **Answer: C — ≈ 160 m**

📝 **Explanation:** For a single-lane road with two-way traffic, two vehicles approaching from opposite directions must both be able to stop. Therefore:

Required SSD = 2 × SSD (for one vehicle)

SSD for 60 km/h (as per IRC) = 80 m. So required SSD = 2 × 80 = **160 m** (option C ≈ 164 m when calculated precisely using the formula).

💡 **Note:** Memorise IRC code values — SSD for 60 km/h = 80 m (with reaction time 2.5 s and appropriate friction). For two-way single-lane roads, always double the SSD.

The retardation formula is: a = f·g

**Q6: Two cars are moving in opposite directions at 25 m/s and 20 m/s on a single-lane road. Reaction time = 2.5 s, f = 0.5. Find the minimum sight distance to avoid a head-on collision.**

✅ **Answer: D — ≈ 258.9 m**

📝 **Explanation:** Since both vehicles are moving at different speeds in opposite directions, each needs its own SSD:

Required Sight Distance = SSD₁ + SSD₂

Calculate SSD₁ (for v₁ = 25 m/s) and SSD₂ (for v₂ = 20 m/s) using SSD = vt + v²/(2gf), then add them. The sum gives approximately **258.9 m**.

**Q7: A vehicle travelling at 60 km/h on a downward gradient observes an obstruction 200 m ahead and brakes. Brake efficiency = 60%, f = 0.35, reaction time = 2.5 s, g = 9.8 m/s². Find the distance required to stop.**

✅ **Answer: D — ≈ 75–79 m range**

📝 **Explanation:** For a gradient with brake efficiency, the general SSD formula is:

SSD = (V × t)/3.6 + V² / (254 × (f·η ± G))

Where η = brake efficiency (decimal), G = gradient (positive for uphill, negative for downhill), V in km/h.

For a **downward** gradient, use the **minus** sign (the gradient aids motion and increases stopping distance).

Substitute V = 60, t = 2.5, f = 0.35, η = 0.6, G = 0.01 → **Answer ≈ 75–79 m (Option D).**

**Q8: Design speed = 60 km/h, f = 0.36, reaction time = 2.5 s, g = 9.8 m/s². Find the Intermediate Sight Distance (ISD).**

- (A) 80 m
- (B) 162 m
- (C) 120 m
- (D) 240 m

✅ **Answer: B — 162 m**

📝 **Explanation:** ISD = 2 × SSD. SSD for 60 km/h = 80 m (IRC value). Therefore ISD = 2 × 80 = **160 m ≈ 162 m (Option B).**

💡 **Note:** The relationship between sight distances — SSD : ISD : OSD is approximately 1 : 2 : 8.

**Q9: A vehicle decelerates from 20 m/s to 10 m/s on a road with gradient +4%. f = 0.35, g = 9.8 m/s². Find the braking distance.**

📝 **Explanation:** When the vehicle does not stop completely but reduces speed from v₁ to v₂, the braking distance formula modifies to:

BD = (v₁² − v₂²) / (2g(f ± G))

Where v₁ = 20 m/s (initial), v₂ = 10 m/s (final), f = 0.35, G = 0.04.

For an **upward** (+) gradient: use **plus** sign (gradient opposes motion, reduces stopping distance).

BD = (400 − 100) / (2 × 9.8 × (0.35 + 0.04)) = 300 / (2 × 9.8 × 0.39)

💡 **Note:** The key difference from a standard braking distance problem is that v_final ≠ 0. Use BD = (v₁² − v₂²) / (2gf) for level road; add ±G in the denominator for gradient.

**Q1: A vehicle is overtaking a truck. Both initially travel at 10 m/s with a spacing of 16 m. The car accelerates at 4 m/s². After overtaking, the car maintains the same 16 m clearance ahead of the truck. What is the total distance covered by the car during the overtaking maneuver?**

- (A) 56 m
- (B) 72 m
- (C) 88 m
- (D) 96 m

✅ **Answer: B — 72 m**

📝 **Explanation:** The overtaking distance formula is:

**d = vB × T + 2S**

where vB = speed of the slow vehicle (truck) = 10 m/s, S = spacing between vehicles = 16 m, and T = overtaking time.

T is found using: **T = √(2S / a)** = √(2 × 16 / 4) = √8 = **4 seconds** (approximately, with the factor working out cleanly here).

Substituting: d = (10 × 4) + (2 × 16) = 40 + 32 = **72 m**

💡 **Note:** For OSD problems in exams, a complete OSD calculation is unlikely. Focus on these two key equations: **T = √(2S/a)** and **d = vB·T + 2S**. The full OSD is simply not examined as a complete problem.

Solving for t gives approximately **2.5 seconds**. Only t is unknown; all other values are given. The answer is **Option A**.

**Q5: A curve changes radius gradually from infinity to a finite value. What type of curve is this?**

✅ **Answer: Transition Curve**

📝 **Explanation:** A transition curve (also called a spiral curve) is one in which the radius changes gradually from infinity (at the tangent point) to the radius of the circular curve. This gradual change allows smooth introduction of centrifugal force.

**Q6: A road has a design speed of 60 km/h and radius 400 m. Find the design superelevation for mixed traffic conditions (IRC 38).**

- (A) 0.03
- (B) 0.04
- (C) 0.04 (approx)
- (D) 0.05

✅ **Answer: C — e = V² / (225·R)**

📝 **Explanation:** For **mixed traffic / design superelevation** as per IRC, the equation is:

**e = V² / (225 × R)**

Here V = 60 km/h, R = 400 m:

e = 60² / (225 × 400) = 3600 / 90000 = **0.04**

💡 **Note:** This formula **e = V² / (225R)** applies when friction is ignored (mixed traffic condition). When friction is included, use: **e + f = V² / (127R)**.

**Q7: What are the reasons for providing extra widening on horizontal curves?**

- (A) Off-tracking only
- (B) Psychological reason only
- (C) Both off-tracking and psychological reason
- (D) Off-tracking, psychological reason, and visibility

✅ **Answer: D — Both off-tracking and psychological reason**

📝 **Explanation:** Extra widening is provided on curves for exactly **two reasons**: off-tracking (the rear wheels of a vehicle do not follow the same path as the front wheels on a curve) and psychological reason (drivers tend to shy away from the edge, so extra width is needed for comfort and safety).

**Q8: For a road with design speed 80 km/h and camber 3%, beyond what radius is no superelevation required?**

- (A) 1200 m
- (B) 948 m
- (C) 750 m
- (D) 560 m

✅ **Answer: B — 948 m**

📝 **Explanation:** When the calculated design superelevation equals the camber, no superelevation needs to be provided beyond that radius. Substituting camber in place of e in the design superelevation equation:

**R = V² / (225 × e_min)** where e_min = camber = 3% = 0.03

R = 80² / (225 × 0.03) = 6400 / 6.75 = **948 m**

💡 **Note:** The formula for "radius beyond which no superelevation is required" is **R = V² / (225 × camber)**.

**Q11: A two-lane road is 7 m wide with radius 100 m, design speed 50 km/h, and wheel base length 7 m. Find the extra widening required.**

- (A) 0.8 m
- (B) 1.0 m
- (C) 1.2 m
- (D) 1.5 m

✅ **Answer: B — 1.0 m**

📝 **Explanation:** Extra widening formula:

**W_e = n·L² / (2R) + V / (9.5·√R)**

where n = number of lanes = 2, L = wheel base = 7 m, R = 100 m, V = 50 km/h.

W_e = (2 × 49) / 200 + 50 / (9.5 × 10) = 0.49 + 0.526 ≈ **1.0 m**

💡 **Note:** V in this formula is in **km/h**; all other values are in metres. If wheel base is not given, assume **6.0 m** as standard.

**Q14: For a horizontal curve with radius 300 m, design speed 15 m/s, and allowable jerk (rate of change of centrifugal acceleration) C = 0.75 m/s³, find the minimum length of transition curve.**

✅ **Answer: 15 m**

📝 **Explanation:** When C (allowable jerk) is given, use:

**Ls = V³ / (C × R)**

where V is in **m/s**: Ls = 15³ / (0.75 × 300) = 3375 / 225 = **15 m**

💡 **Note:** Three methods exist for finding length of transition curve. When allowable jerk C is given, use **Ls = V³/(C·R)** with V in m/s. If C is not given, it can be found from: **C = 80 / (75 + V)** where V is in km/h. For rate of change of superelevation and extra widening methods, different equations apply.

**Q15: A two-lane road (7.5 m wide) has radius 500 m, design speed 100 km/h. Find the level difference between the inner and outer edges of the road (elevation height h).**

✅ **Answer: Greater than 0.4 m (verify)**

📝 **Explanation:** First find design superelevation using **e = V² / (225 × R)** = 100² / (225 × 500) = 10000 / 112500 ≈ **0.044**. Then find elevation height using:

**h = e × B** (where B = width of pavement = 7.5 m)

h = 0.044 × 7.5 ≈ **0.33 m** (verify with exact question values)

💡 **Note:** The level difference between inner and outer edges = superelevation × pavement width. This is called the **superelevation height** or **elevated height**.
