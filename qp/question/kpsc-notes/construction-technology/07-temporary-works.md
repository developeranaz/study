# 07 — Temporary Works: Shoring, Underpinning, Scaffolding, Formwork

*5 of 66 CT Qs. Definition-discrimination is the whole game here.*

## 7.1 THE Discrimination Table — ⭐ asked 3× (Q6, Q27, Q44)

| Work | Definition | Key word |
|------|-----------|----------|
| **Shoring** | **TEMPORARY structure to support an UNSAFE structure** ⭐ (Q27; Q44 Statement-I called it "permanent" → FALSE) | temporary + lateral/vertical support |
| **Underpinning** | **Strengthening/renewing the FOUNDATION of an existing building** ⭐ (Q6 — remedy for settlement; Q44 Statement-II TRUE) | foundation of EXISTING building |
| **Scaffolding** | Temporary platform for workmen & materials during construction/repair ⚠️ distractor | working platform |
| Timbering | Temporary support to sides of TRENCH excavation ⚠️ distractor (Q6) | trench sides |
| Formwork/centering | Temporary mould for concrete (centering = curved soffits) | mould |

🧠 One-liner: **Shore the WALL, underpin the FOUNDATION, scaffold the WORKMEN, timber the TRENCH, form the CONCRETE.**

## 7.2 Shoring Types & the Flying-Shore Numbers — ⭐ asked

- **Raking shore:** inclined rakers to ground (wall plate, needles, cleats, sole plate; rakers at 45°–75°) 🎯.
- **Flying (horizontal) shore:** between two parallel walls, no ground obstruction; suitable wall gap ≤ 9 m; single flying shore for ~9 m, compound beyond 🎯.
  - ⭐ **Single flying shore: depth ≥ 1/8 of clear span, width ≥ 1/9 of its length** (Q49 — PSC key A).
- **Dead/vertical shore:** vertical dead shores + needles through wall — used when lower part of wall removed (openings, underpinning support) 🎯.

## 7.3 Underpinning Methods 🎯

- **Pit method** (most common: 1.2–1.5 m sections, alternate), pile method (jack/needle piles), wall-in-phases; chemical/grouting stabilization.
- Precautions: work in alternate sections; start from middle; shore the structure first; needles above.

## 7.4 Scaffolding 🎯 (only distractor so far — direct Q likely!)

| Type | Feature |
|------|---------|
| Single (brick-layer's) | ONE row of standards, putlogs into wall holes — brick walls |
| Double (mason's/independent) | TWO rows of standards — stone walls (holes can't be left) |
| Cantilever/needle | Upper-floor work, busy street below |
| Suspended | Platform hung by ropes — painting/repairs |
| Trestle | Movable ladders/tripods ≤ 5 m |
| Steel/tubular | Standard now; couplers, base plates |
| Patented | Ready-made with brackets |

Components 🎯: standards (vertical), ledgers (horizontal ∥ wall), putlogs (transverse, wall↔ledger), braces, guard rail, toe board.

## 7.5 Formwork — ⭐ IS 456 stripping-time question (Q33)

**Minimum period before striking formwork (IS 456:2000, OPC, normal cure):**

| Element | Time |
|---------|------|
| Vertical sides of slabs/beams/columns | 16–24 h |
| Slab soffit (props left) | 3 days |
| **Beam soffit (props left)** | **7 days** ⭐ (Q33) |
| Props to slabs — span ≤ 4.5 m | 7 days |
| Props to slabs — span > 4.5 m | 14 days |
| Props to beams/arches — span ≤ 6 m | 14 days |
| Props to beams/arches — span > 6 m | 21 days |

Ladder mnemonic: **1-3-7 / 7-14 / 14-21.** 🎯 Any row can be the next question — learn the whole table.

- Formwork requirements: rigid, watertight joints, smooth face, easy removal, minimal deflection; camber 4 mm/m to counter deflection 🎯.
- Formwork cost ≈ 20–25% of concrete cost 🎯; shuttering measured in m² (Estimation link).
- Slip forming: continuously moving forms (chimneys, silos) 🎯.

## 7.6 🎯 High-Probability Variations
