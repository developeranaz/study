## 3.1 Brick Positions & Wall Terms — ⭐ asked repeatedly

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Stretcher | Brick with LENGTH parallel to wall face |
| **Header** | **Brick with BREADTH parallel to wall face** ⭐ (Q8) ⚠️ stem says "breadth parallel" → header (the length goes INTO the wall) |
| **Frog** | Depression on brick's top face; ⭐ **laid FROG UP (on its top face)** (Q7) — holds mortar key |
| **Quoin** | **External corner of a wall** ⭐ (Q58) |
| King closer | Brick with one corner cut (half header + half stretcher end) 🎯 |
| Queen closer | Brick halved lengthwise; placed next to quoin header in English bond 🎯 near-certain |
| Bat | Brick cut across width (half/three-quarter bat) 🎯 |
| **Corbel** | **Projecting course(s) from wall face supporting wall plate etc.** ⭐ (Q1) |
| Cornice | Decorative projecting course at roof level ⚠️ distractor |
| Coping | Protective (weathered) topmost course of parapet/compound wall ⚠️ distractor |
| String course | Horizontal decorative course projecting from face 🎯 |
| **Racking back** | **Stepped termination of a wall** ⭐ (Q52) |
| Toothing | Alternate projecting headers left for FUTURE extension ⚠️ trap-pair with racking back |

## 3.2 Bonds — ⭐ TRAP CENTRAL (both asked)

| Bond | Arrangement |
|------|-------------|
| **English bond** | **Headers & stretchers in ALTERNATE COURSES** ⭐ (Q50) — strongest common bond |
| **Flemish bond** | **Headers & stretchers laid ALTERNATELY IN THE SAME COURSE** ⭐ (Q24) — better appearance; single vs double Flemish 🎯 |
| Stretcher bond | All stretchers — only for half-brick walls (partitions) 🎯 |
| Header bond | All headers — curved walls, footings 🎯 |
| **Garden-wall bond** | For **10 cm (one-brick... PSC key: 10 cm) thin boundary walls** ⭐ (Q63 — PSC answer 10 cm; English/Flemish garden-wall variants use 1 header per 3–5 stretchers 🎯) |
| Rat-trap bond | Bricks on edge, cavity inside — economical, thermal 🎯 (Kerala loves cost-effective tech — Laurie Baker!) |
| Zigzag/herring-bone | Paving/brick flooring 🎯 |

**Rules of bonding 🎯:** vertical joints must not be continuous; use closers next to quoin headers; lap ≥ ¼ brick.

## 3.3 Mortar & Strength Facts — ⭐ asked

- ⭐ **Reinforced brickwork (RB) mortar = 1:3** cement mortar (Q5).
- Mortar for ordinary brickwork 1:6; pointing 1:1–1:3; plastering 1:3–1:6 🎯.
- ⭐ **Safe permissible load on coursed rubble in cement mortar = 90 t/m²** (Q9) (≈0.9 MPa).
- ⭐ **Mortar joint thickness, coursed rubble masonry II sort = 12 mm** (Q10) (I sort: 10 mm 🎯).
- Brick masonry joint: 10 mm standard 🎯.

## 3.4 Stone Masonry Types 🎯 (only rubble asked so far — expect more)

- **Rubble masonry:** random (uncoursed/built-to-courses), coursed (I/II/III sort by joint 10/12/16 mm & hammer dressing), polygonal, flint, dry rubble.
- **Ashlar masonry:** fine (chisel-dressed, 3 mm joints), rough-tooled, rock/quarry-faced, chamfered, block-in-course.
- Terms: through stone/bond stone (transverse strength 🎯 likely), cornerstone, plinth course, lacing course.

## 3.5 Pointing — ⭐ asked (Q66)

| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| Flush | Mortar pressed flush with face — most durable 🎯 |
| **Recessed** | **Face kept VERTICAL, pressed inside ~5 mm with tool** ⭐ (Q66) |
| Struck | Face inclined, upper edge pressed IN 10 mm — sheds water 🎯 |
| Keyed/grooved | Groove formed with 6 mm rod 🎯 |
| Tuck | Groove filled with white lime putty projecting 3 mm ⚠️ distractor in Q66 |
| Weathered, V-grooved, beaded | know names 🎯 |
