# 06 — Plane Table Surveying

*KPSC weight: ~16 of 203 Qs (8%). Almost entirely "which method for which situation" — one comparison table wins all marks.*

## 6.1 Basics

- ⭐ Plane tabling = **field work and plotting done SIMULTANEOUSLY** (Q103) — graphical method.
- Advantages: no field book, no plotting mistakes (checked on spot), fast, cheap. Disadvantages: unsuitable in rainy climate (⚠️ relevant to Kerala!), not precise, awkward to carry.
- Accessories: **alidade** (plain/telescopic), trough compass (orientation), spirit level, U-frame/plumbing fork with plumb bob (**centering**), drawing sheet, water-proof cover.
- ⭐ Working/bevelled edge of alidade = **FIDUCIAL edge** (asked 2× — Q183, Q186).
- Setting up = three operations: **levelling, centering, orientation** 🎯 (orientation = keeping table parallel to its previous position — by trough compass or back-sighting; back-sighting more accurate 🎯).

## 6.2 THE Four Methods — ⭐ asked 8+ times, MEMORIZE THIS TABLE

| Method | Table occupies | Use — exact PSC phrases |
|--------|----------------|------------------------|
| **Radiation** | ONE station, rays drawn + distances measured to points | Details **from stations already established by triangulation** ⭐ (Q113); small areas visible from one point; with tacheometer for contouring steep ground |
| **Intersection** | TWO stations (ends of measured base line); rays from both intersect | **Inaccessible / distant objects** ⭐🔁3× (Q88, Q132, Q178); **rivers, broken boundaries** ⭐ (Q114); **hilly country where distances can't be measured** ⭐ (Q140) |
| **Traversing** | Successive stations along a line/route | Survey lines of a closed/open traverse (like compass traverse but graphical) |
| **Resection** | Station whose position is NOT yet plotted | **Locating the station occupied by the table** ⭐🔁3× (Q39, Q117) |

⭐ Methods of plane tabling = **4 distinct heads** (Q171).

⚠️ MASTER TRAP: *"Intersection locates OTHER points; Resection locates YOURSELF."*

## 6.3 Resection & Its Problems — high-frequency zone

- ⭐ **Resectors** = rays drawn back from known plotted points to the unplotted occupied station (Q12).
- Orientation methods for resection: by trough compass, by back sighting, by **two-point problem**, by **three-point problem**.
- ⭐ **Two-point problem:** orienting/locating the table with observations to **TWO points whose positions are already plotted** (Q11 — answer "Two-point problem", Q39 — "location of station by sights to two plotted points"). Requires auxiliary station — laborious.
- **Three-point problem:** locate station by sighting THREE well-defined plotted points. Solutions:
  1. Tracing paper method (mechanical)
  2. **Bessel's graphical solution** ⭐ (Q179 — Bessel's = **graphical method**)
  3. Trial and error (Lehmann's method) — most accurate/rapid; Lehmann's rules 🎯.
- ⭐ Two-point & three-point problems are methods of **"orientation AND resection"** (Q199 answer C).
- **Strength of fix** (three-point): good when station inside great triangle; poor near the **danger circle** (circle through the 3 points — fix indeterminate ON it) 🎯 near-certain future question.

## 6.4 🎯 High-Probability Variations

- Which accessory for centering (plumbing fork) / orientation (trough compass)?
- Telescopic alidade → plane table + tacheometry.
- "Plane table survey is most suitable for ___" → small-scale/medium filling-in of details, open country.
- Errors: non-horizontality of board, defective sighting, orientation error (biggest source).
- Simple definitions: "the intersection of plotted rays gives location of the point" etc.
