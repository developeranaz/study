# 05 — Contouring

*KPSC weight: ~14 of 203 Qs. Small theory set — nearly every fact below has already been asked once.*

## 5.1 Definitions — ⭐ all asked

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Contour line** | Imaginary line joining points of **equal elevation** ⭐ (Q102) |
| **Contour interval (CI)** | **VERTICAL distance between two consecutive contours** ⭐ (Q146) — constant for a given map |
| **Horizontal equivalent (HE)** | Horizontal distance between two consecutive contours — varies with slope |
| **Contour gradient** | Imaginary line on the ground with **constant inclination to horizontal** ⭐ (Q98) |

- ⚠️ TRAP: CI = vertical & constant; HE = horizontal & variable. PSC swaps these in options.
- ⭐ HE depends upon **slope of the ground** (Q40) (CI depends on scale, purpose, funds, terrain).
- CI is inversely proportional to scale: small scale → large interval. Typical: flat 0.25–0.5 m; rolling 0.5–1 m; hilly 1–2 m; steep 2–3 m. 🎯
- Ceylon ghat tracer sets out contour gradient (see file 04).

## 5.2 Characteristics of Contours — ⭐ heavily asked, memorize ALL

1. Two contours of **different** elevations **cross only at an OVERHANGING CLIFF** ⭐ (Q118).
2. Contours of different elevations **unite/merge only at a VERTICAL CLIFF** ⭐🎯.
3. Two contours of the **same** elevation **cannot cross** (and don't merge) ⭐ (Q107 — "cannot cross each other").
4. **Equally spaced parallel contours → UNIFORM slope** ⭐ (Q14). ⚠️ distractor "plane surface": a plane surface gives straight, parallel, equidistant contours — PSC's Q14 answer was "uniform slope"; if "straight AND parallel AND equidistant" appears, that's a PLANE.
5. Close spacing → **steep** slope; wide spacing → **gentle** slope 🎯.
6. **Higher values INSIDE the loop → RIDGE/hill** ⭐ (Q55); lower values inside → depression/pond/valley.
7. Ridge line: contours cross it at right angles, convexity toward lower ground (U shapes pointing away from high ground); **valley line: V-shaped contours pointing toward higher ground** 🎯 classic next question.
8. A contour must close on itself (within or outside the map); cannot end abruptly.
9. A single contour cannot split into two (except cliff cases).

## 5.3 Methods of Contouring

**Direct method:** instrument locates points ON the contour itself (trace each contour).
- ⭐ Locating points on the contour = **VERTICAL control** (Q15); locating their plan positions = horizontal control.
- Slow, tedious, but most **accurate**; suits small areas. 🎯

**Indirect methods:** levels taken at chosen points, contours **interpolated**.
- ⭐ **Interpolation** = placing contour lines **proportionally between plotted ground points** (Q127). Methods: estimation, arithmetic calculation, graphical.
- **Method of squares (grid):** open, gently sloping land.
- **Method of cross-sections:** ⭐ best for **route/road/railway surveys** (Q70).
- **Radial line (radiating) method:** hilly terrain with a commanding station; ⭐ **for steep & broken ground, tacheometric/RADIATION method is most suitable** (Q100 answer: Radiation).

## 5.4 Uses of Contour Maps 🎯 (likely definitional Qs)

- Nature of terrain (character study), suitable route selection (alignment), inter-visibility of points, catchment area & reservoir capacity, earthwork quantities, site selection (dam/bridge).
- Reservoir capacity from contour areas → use trapezoidal/prismoidal formula on contour areas.
- Grade contour: joining points at the design gradient — road alignment on hills.

## 5.5 🎯 High-Probability Variations

- "Contour interval of a map is 2 m, HE is 20 m → slope = 2/20 = 1 in 10."
- Which method (squares/cross-section/radial) suits which terrain — asked once, will repeat with different terrain.
- Watershed/ridge vs valley recognition from contour description.
- Depression contours shown with hachures inside.
