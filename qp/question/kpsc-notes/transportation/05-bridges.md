# 05 — Bridges (3 Qs)

## Substructure supports — the pier/abutment/crib trio

- ⭐ **PSC FACT (Q33):** Intermediate supports of a **multi-span** bridge superstructure
  = **PIERS**.
- **Abutment** = END support (also retains earth of approach).
- ⭐ **PSC FACT (Q25):** **Cribs** = TEMPORARY pier made in the river bed (timber/steel
  framework filled with stone; used during construction/military bridging).
- **Wing wall** = retains earth beside abutment, flanks the approaches.

⚠️ TRAP: all four (abutment / pier / wing wall / crib) appear together as options.
Keys: intermediate+permanent ⇒ pier; end ⇒ abutment; temporary ⇒ crib; sides ⇒ wing wall.

## Superstructure by span

- ⭐ **PSC FACT (Q10, filed under railways too):** small-span railway bridge ⇒
  **steel (plate) girder**.
- 🎯 Span ladder: slab culvert (<6 m) → T-beam (10–25 m) → plate girder (10–30 m rail) →
  truss (30–100+ m) → arch → cable-stayed → suspension (longest, road only).

## 🎯 Likely neighbourhood facts (not yet asked)

- Culvert = bridge with total length ≤ 6 m; minor bridge ≤ 60 m; major bridge > 60 m.
- **Afflux** = rise in upstream water level due to bridge obstruction.
- **Freeboard** = HFL to formation level margin; vertical clearance = HFL to soffit.
- Scour depth (Lacey) governs foundation depth; wells/caissons for deep river foundations.
- Economical span: cost of superstructure = cost of substructure.
- Bearings transfer superstructure load to pier (rocker/roller — one fixed, one free).
- Kerb = raised edge of deck; scuppers = deck drainage openings (both were distractors in Q25).
