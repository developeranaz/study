# 04 — Bricks & Clay Products

*11 of 79 Qs. Brick-earth composition percentages are the core; the Q1 match-list covers five facts in one.*

## 4.1 Composition of Good Brick Earth — ⭐ heavily asked

| Ingredient | Good % | Excess causes (⭐ Q1 match-list) |
|-----------|-------:|-------------------------------|
| **Silica** | **50–60%** ⭐🔁 (Q6; Q24 was the same Q corrupted) | Destroys cohesion → brick **brittle** |
| **Alumina (clay)** | **20–30%** ⭐ (Q9) | **Shrinks & warps** during drying/burning |
| Lime | < 5 | **Melts & loses shape** (over-burnt lime also causes lime-blowing/unsoundness) |
| Iron oxide | 5–6 | Makes brick **dark blue** (right amount → red colour) |
| Magnesia | < 1 | **Decay** of bricks / yellow tint |

- ⭐ Q1 answer code: alumina→shrink/warp, silica→brittle, lime→melt, iron oxide→dark blue, magnesia→decay (option B). Memorize the effects, not the code letters.
- 🎯 Harmful ingredients: lime nodules, iron pyrites, alkalies (efflorescence), pebbles, organic matter (bloating).

## 4.3 Frog & Manufacture

- ⭐ **Frog = key for holding mortar** (Q30). ⚠️ TRAP distractor "to indicate manufacturing details" — manufacturer's name IS stamped in the frog, but its FUNCTION is mortar key. Laid frog UP.
- 🎯 Manufacture sequence: preparation (unsoiling→digging→cleaning→weathering→blending→**tempering** Q59) → moulding (ground/table; hand/machine) → drying → burning (clamp or kiln; Bull's trench, Hoffman's continuous kiln).

## 4.4 Brick Bonds (Q77 landed in BM)

- ⭐ **English bond = ALTERNATE courses of headers and stretchers** (Q77) — strongest common bond.
- 🎯 Flemish bond: header & stretcher alternate IN THE SAME course (better appearance, less strength). Header bond: all headers (curved walls). Stretcher bond: half-brick walls/partitions. ⚠️ classic English-vs-Flemish trap.

## 4.5 Clay Products

- ⭐ **Porcelain = fine earthenware, white, thin, SEMI-TRANSPARENT** (Q78). ⚠️ vs terracotta (burnt clay, brown/red, ornamental), stoneware (hard, opaque, glazed pipes), earthenware (porous, coarse).
- 🎯 Glazing types (salt glazing for stoneware), fire-clay/refractory bricks (withstand high temp, high alumina).
